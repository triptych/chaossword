# Chapter 10: Blood Tides

Blackwood handed out ceremonial robes to each of them, her eyes gleaming with satisfaction. Lyra ran her fingers over the midnight blue fabric of hers, noticing the crimson threads that formed patterns along the sleeves and hem. The symbols looked familiar—like the ones from the hidden tablet, but changed in subtle ways.

"These will make your powers stronger," Blackwood explained. "I had them specially made by weavers who know the old ways."

«Those symbols are dangerous,» Kais warned in Lyra's mind. «They're meant to channel power, but Blackwood changed them to funnel everything to herself instead of keeping the elements separate.»

*Just another way she's twisting everything,* Lyra thought, keeping her face blank as she examined the robe. Every step Blackwood took made it clearer she wanted all the artifacts' power for herself—exactly what the tablet had warned against.

"Before our ritual," Blackwood continued, unrolling a map across the table, "we have one more task." She pointed to a coastal settlement nestled in a sheltered bay. "Rockhollow Harbor—home to the Stonefist tribe. Their elders have knowledge about earth artifacts, and more importantly, they have a warrior whose blood makes him perfect for the Root."

Vex leaned forward, blue light from the Chalice reflecting in his eyes. "Another bearer? We already have the boy." He gestured toward Tam, who looked worse than before. Stone-like patterns now covered his forearms permanently.

"The boy is weak," Blackwood said dismissively. "He fights the bonding too much, making the artifact unstable. We need someone with natural earth energy." Her finger tapped the map. "Their champion, Thorn, comes from the original Guardian bloodline that protected the Root of Stone. With him properly bonded, our ritual will be much more likely to succeed."

Ilaria twirled the Feather between her fingers. "The village doesn't have strong defenses. They rely on their terrain and reputation for earth magic to keep enemies away. Their warriors are strong in close combat but have few ranged weapons."

Lyra felt sick listening to this cold assessment. Another village about to be destroyed for Blackwood's ambition—just like Stillwater.

"We attack at midnight," Blackwood declared. "Our goals are simple: capture the warrior Thorn and eliminate anyone who might follow us before the ritual. Speed and precision are key."

As they went over the plans, Lyra's mind raced. The attack would be just like what happened to her village. Worse, she would be part of it—no longer just a victim but helping destroy another community.

«This might actually help us,» Kais said thoughtfully. «The chaos of battle could give us a chance to act.»

*What can we possibly do?* Lyra asked silently. *We're surrounded by enemies on their ships.*

«Not to escape—to talk to Thorn. If he really is connected to the original guardians, he might know how to properly bond with artifacts.»

When the meeting ended, Blackwood assigned Lyra to create protective barriers for key crew members during the raid—keeping her close to the captain throughout the attack.

As they left to prepare, Nari fell into step beside Lyra, whispering, "This is wrong. Attacking innocent people who've done nothing wrong."

"Just like they did to Stillwater," Lyra agreed grimly. "Another village sacrificed for artifacts."

They found a quiet spot on the bow where the wind and waves would hide their conversation. Nari glanced around nervously. "There's something else. The Stonefist tribe has old alliances with the Coastal Federation. Attacking Rockhollow will unite all the neutral settlements against Blackwood's fleet."

"How do you know this?"

"I studied the politics of this region before my father's ship was taken. The Stonefist are respected mediators between tribes and kingdoms." Nari's expression darkened. "Blackwood knows this. She's deliberately cutting ties that might challenge her power after the ritual."

That fit with everything Lyra knew about Blackwood. Not content with magical power, she was systematically eliminating any political opposition.

"We need to warn them somehow," Lyra whispered.

Nari shook her head. "We can't before the attack. But maybe..." She paused. "If we get a chance during the raid, we could try to tell their leaders what's really happening and what comes next."

It wasn't much of a plan, but it was better than doing nothing. As night fell, Lyra prepared herself, determined to prevent as much harm as possible during the coming attack.

---

The fleet approached Rockhollow under cover of darkness. Lanterns were put out and sails adjusted for silent movement. Three ships would carry out the raid while others waited to catch any reinforcements.

From the lead ship, Lyra watched the sleeping village through the mist. Unlike Stillwater's wooden buildings, Rockhollow's homes were partly built into cliff faces, with stone walls that would be harder to destroy. Warm light glowed from scattered windows, the people inside unaware of the danger approaching.

«Their name fits,» Kais observed. «The harbor was shaped by earth magic—they moved the stone to create a safe place for ships.»

Blackwood appeared beside Lyra, wearing battle leathers instead of her usual captain's coat. "You'll create shields for our advance team," she instructed. "Focus on blocking their weapons and securing our escape path. Don't waste energy destroying things—we're here to grab specific targets, not kill everyone."

The order surprised Lyra until she realized Blackwood's thinking. The captain didn't care about innocent lives—she just knew that every death would make retaliation from allied settlements more likely.

Small boats were lowered silently into the water, each carrying teams with specific jobs. Lyra found herself in Blackwood's boat with a dozen elite crew members. The sword hummed against her back, its energy building despite their growing partnership.

«Old habits are hard to break,» Kais admitted when Lyra noticed its eagerness. «Centuries of violence leave deep marks. But I see the problem now—how the thirst for blood goes against what I was made for.»

This admission showed how far they'd come. The sword no longer blindly demanded killing but recognized the conflict within itself.

As they neared the shore, Lyra spotted movement along the village edge—guards keeping watch despite the settlement's reputation for being impregnable. They hadn't seen the approaching raiders yet, but discovery seemed only minutes away.

Blackwood signaled to speed up, abandoning stealth for speed as they closed in on the shore. A shout of alarm rang out, followed by stone striking stone—some kind of warning system.

"Shields now," Blackwood commanded as their boat hit the beach.

Drawing the sword, Lyra channeled protective energy like she had during the storm. Blue-gold light spread outward, forming a see-through shield around their landing party. It felt easier now, her body getting used to channeling this power.

Defenders shouted across the harbor as warriors emerged from stone dwellings with surprising speed. Unlike the unprepared villagers of Stillwater, these people responded with organized defense that showed generations of training.

The first volley of stones—launched from sling-like weapons—shattered against Lyra's barrier, falling harmlessly to the ground. Behind her shield, Blackwood's raiders advanced toward their targets.

«Their weapons have earth magic in them,» Kais noted. «The stones are enhanced to hit harder. Clever for people who don't work with metal.»

More raiding parties landed at different points around the harbor, splitting the defenders' attention. Blackwood pushed forward toward the center of the settlement, where their information suggested the tribal leaders and champion would be.

Maintaining the shield tired Lyra as they advanced, especially when attacks intensified. Unlike the passive shield during the storm, this barrier needed constant adjustment to cover their moving group while blocking incoming attacks.

"Extend the shield to the right," Blackwood ordered as they entered the narrow streets of the village.

Lyra obeyed, though each expansion thinned the barrier's power. She could either cover everyone with a weaker shield or protect fewer people with a stronger one.

The village warriors fought them with a distinct style—patient, grounded movements with powerful, focused strikes. Even Blackwood's experienced raiders struggled against defenders who seemed to draw strength from the stone beneath their feet.

«They have natural earth power,» Kais explained. «These warriors connect with their element without formal training.»

Everything changed when a massive figure emerged from the central meeting hall—a man standing nearly seven feet tall, with broad shoulders and intricate tattoos marking him as someone important. The surrounding warriors immediately moved to protect him.

"Thorn," Blackwood said, satisfaction in her voice. "Capture him, but don't hurt him."

The giant warrior carried no visible weapon, yet Blackwood's raiders hesitated to approach. Lyra soon saw why—as Thorn moved, the stone street beneath him responded, ripples flowing outward with each step. Without any obvious spell-casting, he influenced the ground around him just by being there.

«He has a natural connection to the Root's power,» Kais observed. «Blackwood chose well. His compatibility is stronger than any bearer I've seen in centuries.»

This would make him valuable for Blackwood's ritual—but might also give them an opportunity. Could Thorn's understanding of earth magic help them learn how to properly bond with artifacts?

As the fighting intensified, Lyra maintained her shield while watching the villagers with growing discomfort. Not just warriors but families fled from the advancing raiders—children clutching treasured possessions, elders supported by younger family members. These scenes were horrifically familiar from what happened at Stillwater.

The sword felt her distress, its energy fluctuating. «This bothers you deeply,» it acknowledged. «It reminds you of your own trauma.»

*We're the raiders now,* Lyra thought. *We're destroying homes and lives.*

For the first time, Kais didn't try to justify or dismiss her moral concern. Instead, she felt hesitation through their bond—the sword experiencing its own conflict between battle-hunger and growing conscience.

«We don't need to cause more destruction,» it finally suggested. «The shield can stay defensive rather than offensive.»

This showed how much their partnership had evolved. Weeks ago, Kais would have demanded blood without question. Now it suggested restraint in the middle of battle.

Their internal conversation was interrupted as Blackwood's group converged on Thorn's position. The tribal champion had been gradually cut off from supporting warriors through careful maneuvering. Despite his incredible skill, he now faced too many enemies without help.

"Shield here," Blackwood commanded, pointing to a position that would block Thorn's retreat while letting raiders approach from multiple directions.

Lyra obeyed, but subtly changed the shield's configuration—making it protect against deadly attacks from both sides. Blackwood didn't notice the adjustment, focused as she was on capturing Thorn.

The confrontation reached its peak as Thorn realized he was trapped. Instead of surrendering, he pressed his hands against the stone street. The ground trembled, cracks spreading outward as he channeled earth energy with amazing control.

«Impressive,» Kais commented as sections of stone rose to create barriers between Thorn and his attackers. «He taught himself this but performs it with natural skill.»

Blackwood had anticipated this defense. At her signal, Ilaria stepped forward, the Feather glowing between her fingers. The air around Thorn distorted as Ilaria used the Feather's power—not a physical attack but a mental one, breaking his concentration.

The effect was immediate and devastating. Thorn grabbed his head, his earth energy fading as his focus shattered. The stone barriers he'd created crumbled back to street level, leaving him vulnerable as raiders closed in with ropes and restraints.

Even disoriented, Thorn fought with incredible strength, requiring six crew members to secure him. Throughout the struggle, Blackwood watched with cold satisfaction, occasionally glancing at Tam's container where the Root of Stone pulsed with increasing intensity as its intended bearer came near.

With their main target secured, Blackwood signaled retreat. "Fall back to extraction points. Bring the champion and any elders who know about artifact lore."

The raid's precision became clear as raiding parties disengaged simultaneously across the settlement. Unlike the extended destruction of Stillwater, this attack maintained focused objectives—capturing specific targets while avoiding extended fighting that might unite regional forces against them.

As they withdrew toward the harbor, Lyra maintained her shield while struggling with growing guilt. Participating in this raid, however unwillingly, made her complicit in violence against innocent people—exactly what she had suffered herself.

«But your presence saved lives,» Kais noted, sensing her internal struggle. «Your shield protected raiders, yes, but also prevented lethal force against defenders many times. Without you, Blackwood's crew would have killed far more people.»

That offered little comfort, but acknowledged an important truth—she had reduced harm, even if she couldn't prevent it entirely.

Their withdrawal proceeded smoothly until they reached the harbor shore, where unexpected resistance appeared. Village elders—ten figures in ceremonial robes—stood in formation between the raiders and their boats, arms raised in unison.

The ground beneath the elders glowed with embedded patterns that pulsed in rhythm with their synchronized movements. Though outnumbered and facing heavily armed opponents, they projected a unified purpose that made even veteran raiders pause.

"The Stone Circle," Blackwood identified with unexpected respect. "Tribal mages who maintain earth magic traditions."

«They're guardians,» Kais added. «Not warriors but keepers of boundaries—actually similar to my original purpose.»

The elders began a deep chant, their voices creating vibrations that traveled through the harbor stone. Beneath the raiders' feet, the ground softened momentarily before hardening again, temporarily trapping their boots and stopping their movement.

Blackwood's expression hardened as she turned to Ilaria. "Break their concentration. We need to get through."

The Feather bearer stepped forward, her artifact glowing brightly as she directed its power toward the elders. Unlike the focused attack used against Thorn, this power spread widely, trying to shatter the elders' unified consciousness.

The mental assault caused visible distress among the older mages, their chant faltering as Ilaria's power invaded their thoughts. But rather than breaking apart, they moved closer together, supporting those most affected while maintaining their position between raiders and escape.

"Impressive resistance," Blackwood acknowledged before turning to Lyra. "We need more direct action. Use the sword's offensive powers—break their formation."

The command created immediate conflict for Lyra. She had justified creating barriers as primarily defensive, minimizing harm while doing what she was ordered to do. Directly attacking tribal elders defending their home crossed a moral line she wasn't willing to cross.

«We have another option,» Kais suggested, surprising Lyra with its continued restraint. «The shield can be extended like a wedge, separating rather than harming.»

This approach would fulfill Blackwood's tactical need without inflicting direct violence on the elders. Lyra seized this compromise, adjusting her technique to project the barrier forward in a new shape.

The blue-gold energy advanced like a plow blade, gradually separating the elder formation without direct harm. Those caught in its path became disoriented but weren't physically injured as they were gently but firmly pushed aside to create an escape corridor.

Blackwood noticed this adaptation with narrowed eyes but didn't object as the tactic proved effective. Raiders rushed their prisoners toward waiting boats, Thorn's massive form requiring special handling as he continued resisting despite restraints.

As they loaded their vessels, one elder broke from the disrupted formation, rushing toward them with surprising speed for her advanced age. Rather than attacking, she locked eyes with Lyra, recognition flickering in her weathered face.

"Sword bearer," she called in accented common tongue. "The boundary weakens. Four must balance or all collapse."

Before guards could stop her, the elder pressed something into Lyra's palm—a small stone disk etched with symbols matching those from the hidden tablet. The connection was clear: these elders knew about the artifacts' true purpose.

Instinctively closing her fingers around this gift, Lyra maintained her barrier as raiders completed their withdrawal. The elder made no attempt to escape, stepping back with dignity as boats pushed off from shore.

«She recognized us,» Kais said with wonder. «Not just me, but our partnership. The stone contains knowledge—memories preserved in physical form.»

As their vessels retreated into darkness, Lyra looked back at the harbor now lit by torches and lanterns. Unlike Stillwater's complete destruction, Rockhollow remained largely intact—buildings standing, most inhabitants alive though injured and missing their champion and knowledge-keepers.

The relatively contained damage gave little comfort against her participation in violence. However unwilling, she had helped Blackwood capture another victim for her ritual.

---

Dawn broke as their vessels rejoined the main fleet near the Veiled Islands. Despite the raid's success, tension filled the *Tempest* as they secured their new prisoner in the ship's hold.

Thorn's resistance continued during transport, requiring shifts of guards to control him. Unlike Tam's passive struggle with the Root, the tribal champion actively defied his captors—testing restraints, trying to communicate with guards, conserving energy for potential opportunity.

Lyra watched these efforts with growing respect and cautious hope. If anyone might understand proper artifact bonding without corruption, a descendant of original guardians seemed most likely.

While Blackwood met with her officers to finalize ritual preparations, Lyra sought a chance to examine the elder's gift privately. Finding a quiet moment near the ship's stern, she opened her palm to reveal the stone disk.

Intricate patterns covered both surfaces, arranged in concentric circles around a central symbol representing balanced elements. When she rotated the disk, catching morning light at specific angles, certain patterns seemed to shift slightly—revealing hidden information layered beneath the primary engraving.

«Clever,» Kais observed appreciatively. «Multiple layers of information accessible depending on how you look at it. The elders preserved knowledge against those who would suppress it.»

As Lyra turned the disk, the pattern relationships gradually became meaningful—not written language but symbolic communication that worked across any dialect. The outer ring detailed proper elemental relationships between artifacts, confirming what they'd learned from the hidden tablet. But the inner circles contained new information—activation sequences for artifact purification.

«This is what we were missing,» Kais realized. «Not just correct positioning but proper activation order. Water initiates, earth stabilizes, air connects, and fire—chaos—completes transformation.»

This knowledge completed their understanding of how the corrupted ritual might be corrected. If they got a chance during Blackwood's ceremony, they now knew the missing sequence needed for proper implementation.

Their discovery was interrupted by Nari's hushed arrival. "Blackwood's called all artifact bearers to her cabin," she whispered. "Something about 'optimizing resonance' before the ritual."

Lyra hid the stone disk inside her clothing before following Nari toward the captain's quarters. As they moved below decks, they passed the secure hold where Thorn was detained. Through the small observation port, Lyra glimpsed the tribal champion sitting in a meditative pose despite his restraints, looking purposeful rather than defeated.

Inside Blackwood's cabin, the air seemed to hum with energy. The captain had arranged all four artifacts on a small table—chalice, feather, and root positioned around an empty space clearly meant for the sword. Vex and Ilaria already stood at attention, their expressions unnaturally calm, suggesting deepening artifact influence.

"The constellation approaches perfect alignment," Blackwood announced. "Before final preparations, we must maximize harmonic resonance between artifacts and bearers."

She gestured toward the displayed artifacts. "Each element requires specific attunement techniques. The texts describe a preliminary ritual to enhance bonding efficiency before the primary ceremony."

«She's lying,» Kais warned privately. «This 'preliminary ritual' appears nowhere in authentic Telasian texts. She's making this up to serve her purpose.»

Blackwood approached Lyra directly. "Your sword has shown remarkable evolution since our journey began. The protective manifestation during the storm and raid suggests untapped potential valuable to our endeavor."

Her gaze moved meaningfully toward the sword at Lyra's back. "Place Kaisidor with its siblings for preliminary harmonization."

The use of the sword's true name—which Blackwood could only have learned through Ilaria's thought-reading abilities—raised immediate alarm. Blackwood knew more than Lyra had deliberately revealed, suggesting intensifying surveillance.

With careful neutrality, Lyra removed the sword from its harness and approached the arrangement. As she moved to place Kais in the indicated position, subtle warmth flowed through their connection—a warning.

«Trap,» the sword conveyed with certainty. «The configuration is meant for binding, not harmonization. She's trying to weaken our bond so she can take me.»

Understanding bloomed immediately. This "preliminary ritual" was actually Blackwood's attempt to prepare for severing their connection—creating vulnerability she could exploit to claim the sword for herself.

Decision formed instantly between wielder and weapon. Lyra placed Kais in the arrangement but maintained physical contact, one finger resting lightly on the pommel as she stepped back the minimum necessary distance. This subtle resistance went unnoticed as Blackwood began intricate ritual movements around the table.

"Elements divided create boundaries," she intoned, reciting phrases that sounded ancient but contained subtle modern insertions. "Boundaries dissolved create potential. Potential harnessed creates dominion."

The final word—dominion rather than balance—revealed Blackwood's true intent. As she continued, the artifacts began responding to proximity and invocation, each manifesting elemental properties. The chalice rippled like liquid though it held none. The feather lifted slightly without wind. The root pulsed with earth energy that vibrated through the cabin floor.

And Kais—the sword's energy fluctuated between red aggression and blue-gold protection, its nature in flux as opposing purposes contested within its ancient consciousness.

Through their maintained connection, Lyra sensed Blackwood's ritual exerting pressure on their bond—not severing but weakening, creating tiny fractures in the blood magic that united them. The sensation felt like thousands of microscopic threads stretching to breaking point.

«Resist but don't show it,» Kais advised as discomfort intensified. «She expects weakness but doesn't realize we understand what she's doing.»

Maintaining outward composure while internally reinforcing their bond required immense concentration. Lyra focused on memories they now shared—the storm protection, dream communications, moments of genuine partnership rather than coercion. These shared experiences represented evolution beyond the corrupted bonding Blackwood sought to manipulate.

The ritual continued for nearly an hour, Blackwood's invocations growing more elaborate as she wove together authentic phrases with her own modifications. Throughout this performance, Vex and Ilaria remained unnaturally compliant, their wills clearly overcome by artifact influence.

When the ritual finally concluded, Blackwood appeared momentarily tired but satisfied as she studied the artifact bearers. "The preliminary attunement is complete. We proceed to the Convergence Chamber at midnight as planned."

She approached Lyra directly, eyes calculating as she looked for evidence her ritual had worked. "You'll find your connection more... manageable now. The sword's influence properly contained for optimal channeling during tomorrow's ceremony."

The deliberate misdirection confirmed their suspicions. Blackwood believed she had succeeded in weakening their bond, creating vulnerability to exploit during the main ritual. This misconception might give them an advantage if it led her to underestimate their resistance.

As they were dismissed, Lyra retrieved the sword with careful casualness, maintaining the illusion that their connection had been affected as Blackwood intended. Once outside the captain's cabin, she exchanged meaningful glances with Nari before seeking private space to assess their situation.

Alone in her small quarters, Lyra finally allowed herself to examine the strain from Blackwood's ritual. Their bond remained intact but showed stress fractures—not enough to break them apart but concerning nonetheless.

«She knows more than we thought,» Kais acknowledged. «The ritual contained elements from multiple magical traditions, combined effectively. Her preparation has been more thorough than just collecting artifacts.»

"She plans to take you during the main ceremony," Lyra whispered, certainty growing as she processed the implications. "When all artifacts are actively channeling, she'll use whatever method she's designed to break our bond completely."

«Makes sense,» the sword agreed. «With the tribal champion properly bonded to the Root, all four artifacts would be connected to compatible wielders—then centralized under her control through ritual modification.»

The plan's ruthless efficiency was undeniable. And yet, this very ambition created their opportunity. The convergence of all four artifacts—exactly what the hidden tablet and elder's disk described as necessary for purification—would happen because of Blackwood's own actions.

Their planning was interrupted by commotion from below decks—shouting followed by the sound of splintering wood. Guards rushed toward the secured hold where Thorn was detained.

Lyra followed cautiously, staying back while observing the disturbance. The tribal champion had partially broken through his restraints, dislodging the heavy door of his cell though not fully escaping. Six guards struggled to subdue him as Blackwood arrived, her expression coldly furious.

"Bring the Root," she commanded. "Accelerate the bonding process."

A crewmember hurried forward with Tam's containment vessel, now pulsing with intensified earth energy in proximity to its intended bearer. Blackwood removed the twisted root-like artifact, its surface glowing with dull amber light as she approached the restrained champion.

"Your resistance is predictable but ultimately futile," she told Thorn with clinical detachment. "Your bloodline makes you compatible with this artifact regardless of your personal feelings. Accelerated bonding merely hastens inevitable connection."

The tribal champion's response came in his native language—words Lyra couldn't understand but whose defiant tone needed no translation. Despite being restrained, Thorn projected dignity and resolved resistance that commanded reluctant respect even from the guards holding him.

Blackwood merely smiled at his defiance. "Admirable spirit. The Root will find worthy partnership in you, once initial adjustment concludes."

With sudden movement, she pressed the artifact against Thorn's forearm. The contact created immediate reaction—the champion's skin developing the same stone-like patterns seen on Tam, but with greater intensity and rapid spread. Unlike the boy's gradual deterioration, Thorn experienced accelerated transformation that forced sounds of pain despite obvious effort to maintain composure.

"Return him to secured containment," Blackwood ordered once initial bonding reaction stabilized. "Isolation accelerates dependency on the artifact's presence. By midnight, resistance will be manageable."

As guards dragged the semi-conscious champion back to his reinforced cell, Lyra noticed something others missed—beneath pain and defiance, Thorn's expression contained recognition when he glimpsed the sword at her back. Not fear or confusion but knowing acknowledgment, suggesting familiarity with artifact lore beyond Blackwood's assumptions.

«He knows what I am,» Kais confirmed, picking up Lyra's observation. «More importantly, he recognized our bond's nature. His people preserve knowledge of proper artifact partnership.»

This recognition created potential alliance—if they could establish communication before the ritual. Thorn's understanding of earth artifacts might complement what they had learned about proper elemental balance, creating possibility for coordinated intervention.

Blackwood's voice interrupted these thoughts, addressing assembled crew members with renewed authority. "Final preparations commence immediately. Selected personnel will accompany artifact bearers to the Convergence Chamber at midnight. Remaining crew maintain vigilance against potential interference."

She gestured toward the western horizon where the Veiled Islands' central peak rose against darkening sky. "Centuries of planning culminate tomorrow. The power that created boundaries between realms will soon serve more ambitious purpose."

The grandiose declaration revealed Blackwood's ultimate intent—not merely harnessing artifact power but fundamentally altering the separation between realms that Telasian had established through the original artifacts. Such ambition extended beyond mere dominance to reimagining reality itself.

As crew dispersed to their assignments, Nari appeared at Lyra's side, her expression grave. "I've overheard the navigators. The boundary disturbances are getting worse as ritual time approaches. Ships' instruments malfunction near certain locations, and two scout boats failed to return from perimeter patrol."

This information aligned with the elder's warning about weakening boundaries. The convergence of artifacts near their creation site was already affecting the delicate separation between realms, even before Blackwood's intended ritual.

"We need to reach Thorn somehow," Lyra whispered. "He recognized the artifacts and might know techniques for proper bonding."

Nari considered this challenge with strategic assessment. "I can create a brief distraction during evening meal distribution. The guards change rotation then, creating a gap in supervision."

This slim opportunity might provide their only chance for communication before the ritual commenced. With subtle nods, they separated to avoid drawing attention, each preparing for evening's critical opportunity.

---

Darkness fell over the fleet as preparations continued. Selected crew members loaded ritual components into transport vessels for midnight journey to the island's central peak. Blackwood supervised personally, her usual composure amplified by anticipatory energy that showed in her precise movements and sharp commands.

The evening meal provided the promised distraction. As Nari implemented her plan—"accidentally" spilling hot liquid that diverted guard attention—Lyra slipped toward Thorn's containment cell. The small observation port allowed limited visual access, confirming the champion remained alone inside.

"Guardian descendant," she whispered, using the formal address gleaned from the elder's stone disk. "I seek balanced partnership, not dominance."

Thorn's massive form shifted in the darkness, approaching the small opening. His features appeared strained, the Root's spreading influence visible as stone-like patterns now extending across his chest and neck. Despite obvious discomfort, his eyes remained clear and focused.

"Sword bearer," he acknowledged in accented but fluent common tongue. "The elders foretold your coming. Boundary guardian reborn through fire."

The title meant more than just polite address—it recognized the sword's original purpose rather than its corrupted reputation. Thorn understood Kaisidor's true nature despite centuries of violent history.

"Blackwood is corrupting the unification ritual," Lyra explained rapidly, aware their time was limited. "She wants to centralize all four artifacts rather than restore balance."

Thorn nodded, unsurprised. "The hunger for power repeats throughout history. But true power comes from balanced partnership, not control." He gestured toward his transforming body. "Even now, I'm trying to communicate with the Root rather than fight its presence. Partnership requires mutual respect."

This approach contrasted sharply with Tam's resistance strategy, explaining why the boy had deteriorated while Thorn maintained clarity despite accelerated bonding. Rather than rejecting the artifact outright, he sought conversation with it—exactly the balanced relationship Lyra and Kais had gradually developed.

"The purification ritual requires a specific activation sequence," Lyra continued, sharing knowledge from the elder's disk. "Water initiates, earth stabilizes, air connects, fire completes."

Recognition flashed across Thorn's features. "The Elder Circle's teaching survived. When convergence occurs, true bearers must channel simultaneously—not into central vessel but into proper elemental configuration."

This confirmation validated their understanding while adding crucial perspective from earth artifact traditions. Between the hidden tablet, elder's disk, and Thorn's knowledge, they now possessed complete understanding of proper ritual implementation.

Their exchange was interrupted by approaching footsteps. Thorn spoke rapidly before Lyra retreated: "The Root responds to stability of purpose. When the moment comes, stand firm in your intent without wavering. All four artifacts remember their creation—they seek harmony beneath corruption."

With this final insight, Lyra slipped away moments before guards rounded the corner, resuming her evening duties with practiced casualness that hid the significance of this exchange.

«His guidance matches what we've learned,» Kais noted as they processed this conversation later. «The artifacts retain original purpose beneath corrupted use. Given opportunity, they may respond to proper implementation despite centuries of misuse.»

This possibility represented their best hope for intervention—not preventing Blackwood's ritual entirely but transforming it from within at the critical moment when all artifacts converged.

As midnight approached, final preparations accelerated. Artifact bearers donned ceremonial garments, each reflecting their respective elemental associations. Lyra's robe—midnight blue with crimson accents—had been adjusted following her "training" with protective manifestation, now incorporating symbols supposedly enhancing defensive capabilities.

«There are binding elements hidden within the authentic patterns,» Kais identified after careful examination. «Designed to restrict our connection during critical ritual phases.»
