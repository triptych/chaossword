# Chapter 4: Unwelcome Voices

Sleep had become a luxury Lyra could no longer afford.

Three days had passed since her blood had touched the blade—three days of constant, unwanted company inside her own mind. Even in the rare moments when unconsciousness claimed her, the sword pushed images through her dreams: battlefields strewn with corpses, ancient rituals performed in forgotten temples, faces of past wielders contorted in expressions of power and madness.

She woke gasping from one such vision to find the hold in pre-dawn darkness. Around her, other captives slept in uncomfortable positions, their breathing creating a chorus of soft rasps and occasional whimpers. No one had noticed her distress. No one except the presence that never left.

«Weakness,» the sword commented. «Even your nightmares lack proper violence.»

Lyra pressed her palms against her temples, a futile gesture she couldn't stop repeating despite knowing the voice came from within, not without.

*Leave me alone*, she thought fiercely, directing the words inward as she'd learned to do after too many suspicious glances from fellow prisoners who'd caught her speaking aloud to nothing.

«Impossible,» came the immediate reply, tinged with what might have been amusement. «I've waited centuries for a compatible vessel. Did you think I would simply remain silent now that I've found one?»

Lyra curled tighter against the damp wooden hull, trying to create some illusion of privacy in the crowded hold. The sword's constant presence made even this small space feel suffocating, as if she were being watched while changing clothes or bathing.

«Your discomfort is irrelevant,» the sword continued. «What matters is how thoroughly unprepared you are for the potential I offer. Your combat instincts are nonexistent. Your magical awareness is negligible despite obvious latent ability. And your willingness to kill—» The mental equivalent of a disappointed sigh filtered through her consciousness. «—is embarrassingly absent.»

*I'm not a killer*, Lyra responded, the words forming more clearly now with practice. *I never wanted to be.*

«What you want is inconsequential. What you are becoming is inevitable.»

A sound from across the hold interrupted their exchange—the hatch opening to admit weak gray light. A guard's silhouette appeared against the square of sky.

"Up! Captain's orders—all of you topside!" The command was punctuated by a wooden club striking against the hatch frame.

Around Lyra, captives stirred with varying degrees of reluctance. Some had adapted to the routine of captivity with alarming speed, while others still moved as if in a daze. Marla, who had witnessed Lyra's bonding with the sword, kept her distance now, eyeing her with a mixture of fear and pity.

«This could be an opportunity,» the sword observed as prisoners began climbing the ladder toward daylight. «Strike when they least expect it. I can guide your movements. The guard at the top has a knife poorly secured at his belt.»

*Shut up*, Lyra thought with unexpected ferocity. *I'm not attacking anyone. There are children here who would be punished for my actions.*

The sword's displeasure manifested as a sharp pain behind her eyes. «Your compassion for these weaklings is misplaced. They are already doomed.»

Lyra ignored this as she followed the others up the ladder, squinting as full daylight assaulted her vision. The day was clear, the sky a pristine blue that seemed to mock their captivity. The *Tempest* cut through calm waters, part of a fleet whose other vessels dotted the horizon. Crew members moved purposefully around them, barely sparing glances for the captives now assembled on the main deck.

«Six immediate threats within striking distance,» the sword assessed. «The heavyset man to your right is favoring his left leg. Begin with him.»

*I said no!* Lyra mentally shouted, earning a curious glance from the young woman beside her—a slim, sharp-featured girl perhaps a year or two younger than herself, with a distinctive white streak running through her dark hair.

Before she could look away, the girl spoke in a barely audible whisper. "You're talking to yourself again."

Lyra froze, her heart hammering against her ribs. Had she spoken aloud without realizing it?

"I don't know what you mean," she murmured, keeping her eyes fixed on the planks beneath her feet.

The girl's response was interrupted by the appearance of the officer with the twisted mouth—the same woman who had selected Lyra for treasure-securing duty during the storm. She surveyed the assembled captives with cold efficiency.

"Captain Blackwood has decided you'll earn your keep while aboard," she announced. "You'll be assigned duties according to your capabilities. Those who work well may earn better conditions. Those who resist..." She let the threat hang unfinished in the salt-laden air.

The captives were divided into groups—some sent to the galley to assist the cook, others assigned to cleaning duties, the strongest directed to help with physical labor. Lyra found herself among those selected for laundry duty, along with the sharp-featured girl and three others.

They were led to a section of the lower deck where large wooden tubs waited, already filled with seawater. Bundles of soiled clothing—shirts, trousers, and coats belonging to the crew—were stacked nearby. Their guard, a wiry man with a pockmarked face, gave terse instructions before leaving them to the task, locking the door behind him.

The work was grueling. The salt water stung the cuts and scrapes on Lyra's hands, and the constant bending and scrubbing soon made her back ache. But the physical discomfort provided a welcome distraction from the sword's unrelenting commentary.

«Menial labor,» it sneered. «I was forged to sever corrupted magical connections and defend realm boundaries, not to wash pirates' smallclothes.»

The absurdity of the situation—a supposedly ancient magical artifact complaining about laundry—nearly startled a laugh from Lyra's throat. She caught herself just in time, biting her lip as she plunged another shirt into the tub.

"It gets easier if you stop fighting it," the sharp-featured girl said quietly beside her, wringing water from a pair of trousers.

Lyra's hands stilled. "What?"

The girl glanced around to ensure the others were occupied with their own work before continuing. "The voice. The one you're arguing with when you think no one's watching."

Cold fear washed through Lyra. "I don't know what—"

"Save it." The girl cut her off with surprising authority. "I've seen this before. You've bonded with something, haven't you? Something magical."

«She knows too much,» the sword hissed. «Silence her before she exposes our connection.»

Lyra ignored the sword, studying the girl's face for signs of deception or threat. She found neither, only a frank curiosity tinged with caution.

"Who are you?" Lyra asked finally.

"Nari. Formerly of the *Seaflower*, currently of nowhere desirable." She wrung another garment with more force than necessary. "My father was Captain Kerrigan. Blackwood killed him three years ago and kept me as leverage against his former allies."

The name meant nothing to Lyra, but the sword stirred with recognition.

«Kerrigan of the *Seaflower*,» it mused. «Known for precision raids on merchant vessels. Preferred speed and strategy over brute force. Not without skill.»

"I'm Lyra," she offered in return. "From Stillwater village." The words felt hollow, the place they described no longer existing except in memory.

Nari nodded. "I know. I've been aboard longer than most. I notice things." Her eyes flicked meaningfully to Lyra's midsection, where the sword would be if it were physically present. "Like how you were normal when they first brought you aboard, and now you're having conversations with empty air."

Lyra's instinct was to deny everything, but exhaustion and the desperate need for any ally overwhelmed caution.

"It's a sword," she admitted in a whisper. "In the treasure room. During the storm. I cut my hand, and it... connected to me somehow."

Nari sucked in a sharp breath. "One of the artifacts. Gods below." She leaned closer. "Listen carefully. Don't let Blackwood know. She collects them—magical items. It's become an obsession. If she realizes you've bonded with one..."

"Too late for that warning," a melodic voice interrupted from the doorway.

Both women startled, dropping the laundry back into the tubs. Captain Blackwood stood in the entrance, her tall figure silhouetted against the corridor's lamplight. Behind her loomed two guards, their hands resting on weapons.

«Finally,» the sword purred in Lyra's mind. «The one truly worth killing on this floating prison.»

Blackwood stepped into the room, her movements fluid and controlled. The other prisoners shrank back, pressing themselves against the far wall. Only Nari stood her ground beside Lyra, though her face had gone pale.

"You," Blackwood addressed Lyra directly. "You've been working in my treasure room."

It wasn't a question, but Lyra nodded anyway, her mouth suddenly dry.

"And you found something that spoke to you. That called to your blood." The captain's gaze was unnervingly intense. "Something that now whispers constantly in your mind, offering power in exchange for obedience."

Lyra couldn't suppress a small gasp. The accuracy of the description sent a chill down her spine.

«She knows of our kind,» the sword observed. «She has encountered others like me.»

Blackwood smiled thinly, recognizing Lyra's reaction as confirmation. "Bring her," she ordered the guards. "The rest continue working."

"Captain—" Nari began, taking a half-step forward.

"Stay in your place, Kerrigan's daughter," Blackwood cut her off without looking away from Lyra. "Unless you prefer less comfortable accommodations."

The threat silenced Nari, though her eyes communicated a silent warning to Lyra as the guards moved forward to seize her arms.

"No need for that," Lyra said, finding her voice at last. "I'll come willingly."

«Cooperate for now,» the sword advised, its tone calculating. «Learn what she knows of artifacts. Then we'll plan her execution.»

The corridors of the *Tempest* became a maze as the guards led her deeper into the ship than she'd been before. They ascended a narrow staircase to what must have been the captain's quarters—a surprisingly spacious cabin at the stern, with large windows overlooking the ship's wake.

Unlike the utilitarian nature of the rest of the vessel, Blackwood's private domain reflected wealth and calculated intimidation. Exotic weapons hung on the walls. A massive desk dominated the center, its surface covered with maps and nautical instruments. Glass cases displayed smaller treasures—jewels, figurines, and objects Lyra couldn't identify but which radiated a subtle wrongness that made her skin prickle.

«Objects of power,» the sword identified. «Minor artifacts, most of them. Trinkets compared to what I offer.»

Blackwood dismissed the guards with a gesture, then circled her desk to stand before Lyra. Up close, the captain was even more imposing. Her scar, running from temple to jaw, pulled slightly at one corner of her mouth, giving the impression of a perpetual half-smile.

"So," she said, studying Lyra as one might examine an unexpected but interesting insect. "The Sword of Chaos has chosen you. How... unexpected."

The name sent a ripple of satisfaction through the sword's consciousness. «She knows me by reputation. Good.»

"I didn't choose anything," Lyra replied, struggling to keep her voice steady. "It forced itself on me when my blood touched the blade."

Blackwood laughed, the sound genuinely amused. "That's how they work, girl. Blood and trauma forge the strongest bonds." She turned away, moving to a cabinet inlaid with mother-of-pearl. From it, she withdrew a decanter and poured amber liquid into two glasses. "Drink," she commanded, offering one to Lyra.

«Poison is a possibility,» the sword warned. «Though she seems more interested in us alive than dead.»

Lyra accepted the glass but didn't drink. "What do you want from me?"

"Direct. I appreciate that." Blackwood sipped from her own glass. "What I want is simple. I want the Sword of Chaos. I've spent years searching for the Four Artifacts of Balance. I already possess two."

With calculated casualness, she gestured toward a seemingly empty corner of the cabin. For a moment, Lyra saw nothing. Then, as if responding to its mistress's attention, a silver chalice materialized on a pedestal, its surface rippling like disturbed water despite being solid metal.

«The Chalice of Tides,» the sword identified, an unexpected note of recognition in its mental voice. «My sibling of water and order. It has been... centuries.»

Blackwood noted Lyra's reaction. "Ah, your blade recognizes its old companion. And here—" She indicated an ordinary-looking quill pen resting on her desk. "The Feather of Whispers."

As if hearing its name, the quill twitched slightly, leaving a faint golden trail in the air.

«Another sibling,» the sword acknowledged. «Air and knowledge. Three of us, gathered after so long apart.»

"You understand now," Blackwood continued, circling Lyra like a predator. "I am close to reuniting all four artifacts. Only the Root of Stone eludes me still, and I have promising leads." She stopped directly behind Lyra. "But there's a complication."

The captain's breath was warm against Lyra's ear, making her shiver involuntarily.

"Once bonded to a wielder, artifacts cannot simply be taken. They must be relinquished willingly, or..." She let the implication hang in the air.

«Or the wielder must die,» the sword completed silently. «Though death doesn't always break the stronger bonds.»

"You want me to give you the sword," Lyra said flatly.

Blackwood moved back into view, her expression calculating. "Eventually. For now, I want to understand this unexpected development. The sword is notoriously... particular about its wielders. It has rejected or destroyed dozens of potential candidates I've exposed to it."

"Lucky me," Lyra muttered.

The captain smiled, cold and sharp. "Indeed. The question is why. What makes you—a simple village girl with no apparent magical training—suitable for one of the most powerful artifacts ever created?" She reached out suddenly, grasping Lyra's chin and tilting her face to examine it more closely. "There must be something I'm missing."

The sword's rage flared at the contact, sending a spike of pain through Lyra's temple. «Strike her. Now. My power flows through your blood. Use it!»

The command was so forceful that Lyra's hand actually twitched upward before she regained control. Blackwood noticed the movement and released her, stepping back with newfound wariness.

"Interesting," she murmured. "It protects you. Or perhaps it simply doesn't like others touching its property."

"I'm not property," Lyra snapped, the words emerging before she could consider their wisdom.

Blackwood laughed again. "Tell that to the blade whispering in your thoughts. It's been dominating wielders for centuries." She returned to her desk, setting down her glass. "But I'm getting ahead of myself. You need proper context to understand the position you're in."

She withdrew a rolled parchment from a drawer and spread it on the desk's surface. Lyra recognized it immediately—the same map Blackwood had shown Merina before killing her. The memory sent a fresh wave of grief and anger through her.

«Use that anger,» the sword urged. «Channel it. Let me show you how.»

"The Four Artifacts of Balance," Blackwood explained, gesturing to symbols marked at different points on the map. "Created during the Era of Four Realms to maintain equilibrium between elemental forces. Each represents a fundamental magical principle—chaos, order, knowledge, and stability." She tapped each symbol in turn. "They were separated after the Great Collapse when their combined power proved too dangerous to contain in one location."

Despite herself, Lyra found her attention caught by the history. "Why are you collecting them?"

"Power," Blackwood stated simply. "Not just physical power, though that's considerable, but the power to reshape the very nature of reality." Her eyes gleamed with fervor that bordered on madness. "Imagine controlling the fundamental forces that govern existence. No kingdom could stand against such might. No law would apply. Absolute freedom."

«Her ambition exceeds her understanding,» the sword commented. «The artifacts were never meant for conquest. Such use led to the corruption that now plagues us all.»

This information surprised Lyra. *You were meant for something else?* she directed the thought inward.

«A discussion for another time,» the sword deflected. «When we're not in the presence of one who would misuse us further.»

Blackwood was watching Lyra's face closely. "You're communicating with it now, aren't you? I can see the subtle changes in your expression." She leaned forward, suddenly intense. "What does it tell you about the artifacts' true purpose?"

"Nothing I'm willing to share with you," Lyra replied, surprised by her own boldness.

The captain's expression hardened. "I've been searching for these artifacts for five years. I've sacrificed more than you can imagine to collect them. If you think your temporary bond gives you any leverage here, you're sorely mistaken."

She straightened, composure returning like a mask sliding back into place. "But I'm a practical woman. Fighting your bond would be counterproductive for now. Instead, we'll work with it."

"What does that mean?" Lyra asked, suspicion sharpening her tone.

"It means," Blackwood replied with calculating precision, "that starting tomorrow, you'll be trained in combat. The sword chose you for a reason. Let's discover what that reason is."

«Finally,» the sword's satisfaction pulsed through their connection. «A chance to develop your potential.»

*Don't sound so pleased*, Lyra shot back. *She still plans to separate us eventually.*

«She can try,» the sword responded with cold certainty. «Many have. All have failed.»

"Return her to the general quarters," Blackwood instructed as she opened the cabin door, revealing the waiting guards. "Ensure she's separated from the Kerrigan girl. I don't want them sharing information."

As the guards led Lyra back through the ship's corridors, her mind raced with implications. Blackwood knew far more about the sword than she did—knowledge that might help Lyra understand and potentially control this unwanted bond. But the captain's ultimate goal remained their separation, which the sword seemed confident would prove fatal.

"In here," one guard grunted, opening the door to a small cabin rather than the hold where the other villagers were kept. "Captain's orders."

The space was tiny—barely room for a narrow bunk and small chest—but infinitely better than the crowded prisoner hold. Lyra stepped inside, momentarily confused by this apparent preferential treatment.

"Don't get comfortable," the guard advised, his hand lingering on the door. "Training starts at dawn. And it won't be gentle."

The door closed, followed by the definitive sound of a lock engaging.

«Isolation,» the sword observed. «A standard tactic when dealing with new wielders. Remove support systems, increase dependency on the artifact.»

Lyra sank onto the bunk, suddenly aware of the bone-deep exhaustion she'd been fighting. The thin mattress felt luxurious after days of sleeping on bare planks.

«Rest while you can,» the sword advised, its voice taking on an almost tutoring tone. «Tomorrow begins your true education.»

*I never asked for this*, Lyra thought, closing her eyes against the reality of her situation.

«Few worthy wielders do. Power seeks those who wouldn't abuse it, then ironic ally corrupts them through necessity.» The sword's mental voice carried a weight of experiences Lyra couldn't begin to comprehend. «Sleep. You'll need your strength.»

For once, Lyra didn't argue. Exhaustion claimed her almost immediately, dragging her into dreams filled with battles she hadn't fought and faces she'd never seen—the sword's memories bleeding into her own, erasing the boundaries between them one night at a time.

---

Dawn arrived with a pounding on the cabin door that jolted Lyra from uneasy sleep. The sword had been active in her dreams again, showing her combat techniques performed by previous wielders, their movements fluid and lethal.

"Up!" a voice commanded. "On deck in five minutes."

She rose, feeling the ship's gentle rocking beneath her feet. A pitcher of water and clean clothing had been left sometime during the night—simple trousers and a shirt similar to what the crew wore, rather than her increasingly ragged village dress.

«They're preparing you for your role,» the sword noted. «Creating distance between you and your former identity.»

Lyra changed quickly, uncomfortably aware of the sword's presence even in this private moment. The new clothes fit reasonably well, though they carried the scent of previous owners and harsh soap. She splashed water on her face, trying to clear the fog of disturbed sleep.

The main deck was awash in early morning light when she emerged. Most of the crew were already engaged in their duties, paying her little attention. Near the foremast, a cleared space had been created, with various weapons laid out on a canvas sheet.

Captain Blackwood waited there, no longer wearing her elaborate coat but instead dressed in simple, practical clothing that emphasized her lean, muscular build. Beside her stood a tall man with dark skin and a shaved head, his arms crossed over a broad chest.

«Interesting,» the sword commented. «The captain trains you personally rather than delegating. She's more intrigued by our bond than she admitted.»

Lyra approached cautiously, acutely aware of curious glances from nearby crew members. Whatever was about to happen, it would have an audience.

"You slept well?" Blackwood asked, though her tone suggested she cared little for the answer.

"Well enough," Lyra replied neutrally.

The captain nodded toward the weapons laid out on the canvas. "Choose one."

Lyra stared at the array—knives of various lengths, a short sword, a wooden staff, even what appeared to be a small axe. None called to her; they were just objects, lacking the unsettling sentience of the artifact bound to her.

«The short sword,» the blade advised in her mind. «Similar weight and balance to my physical form. It will prepare your muscles properly.»

Without questioning why she followed its counsel, Lyra reached for the indicated weapon. It felt awkward in her hand, nothing like the perfect balance she'd briefly experienced with the actual sword.

"Interesting choice," Blackwood observed. "Let's see what instincts the artifact has given you." She nodded to the man beside her. "Koric will test your defenses. Try not to get killed."

Before Lyra could process this alarming instruction, the man—Koric—moved toward her with predatory grace, a wooden practice sword in his hand. His first strike was relatively slow, clearly intended to gauge her response rather than cause harm.

Lyra's body reacted before her mind fully registered the attack. She stepped sideways, bringing her borrowed sword up in a defensive motion she had never practiced but somehow knew. The blades connected with a solid *thwack* that reverberated up her arm.

«Better than expected,» the sword acknowledged, «but still pathetic. Your grip is wrong. Feet shoulder-width apart. Weight on the balls of your feet, not the heels.»

The next strike came faster. Lyra managed to block again, but barely, the force driving her back a step.

«You're defending when you should be attacking!» the sword raged. «Strike at his exposed side!»

But Lyra couldn't reconcile the idea of deliberately attacking another person, even in training. She maintained her defensive posture, blocking as best she could while Koric increased the speed and complexity of his attacks.

Within minutes, sweat poured down her face. Her arms trembled with effort. A particularly forceful blow knocked the practice sword from her grip, sending it clattering across the deck.

"Enough," Blackwood called, stepping forward. "Your technique is atrocious, but you shouldn't have any instinct for swordplay at all." Her eyes narrowed. "The artifact is helping you, isn't it? Guiding your movements?"

Lyra nodded, too winded to speak. In truth, the sword had been shouting instructions constantly, growing increasingly frustrated when she failed to follow its more aggressive suggestions.

«You fight its guidance,» Blackwood observed, her perception uncomfortably accurate. "That's why your movements are disjointed. You're caught between its combat knowledge and your own reluctance."

«The pirate understands better than you do,» the sword hissed. «Stop resisting and let me make you formidable.»

*I don't want to be formidable*, Lyra thought back. *I just want to survive.*

«Those are increasingly the same goal,» the sword replied ominously.

Blackwood signaled to a nearby crew member. "Bring the artifact."

Lyra's pulse quickened. Despite the sword's constant mental presence, she hadn't seen its physical form since that moment in the treasure room when blood had sealed their bond.

The crew member returned carrying a wooden box. Blackwood opened it to reveal the Sword of Chaos, still in its plain leather scabbard, looking deceptively ordinary against the velvet lining.

«At last,» the sword's anticipation surged through their connection. «Reunion with my physical form.»

As the box was brought closer, Lyra felt a curious sensation—like a phantom pulse beating in synchronization with her own heart. The sword's mental presence grew stronger, more defined, its satisfaction radiating through their bond.

"Take it," Blackwood commanded.

Lyra hesitated, fear warring with a strange, unwelcome longing. The sword's physical form shouldn't matter when its consciousness was already within her mind, yet she found herself drawn to it nonetheless.

«Embrace what you cannot escape,» the sword urged. «Take up your birthright.»

*It's not my birthright*, she objected, even as her hand moved forward of its own accord.

Her fingers closed around the hilt. Immediately, warmth spread up her arm and across her chest, as if she'd stepped into sunlight after hours in shadow. The sword's presence in her mind clarified, becoming less an invasive voice and more an extension of her own consciousness.

"Now," Blackwood said, stepping back. "Try again."

Koric moved into position, his expression professionally blank despite the unusual situation. He raised his practice sword in silent challenge.

Lyra drew the blade from its scabbard.

In the direct sunlight, it wasn't as plain as she'd first thought. Subtle patterns ran along the metal, reminiscent of flames when caught at certain angles. The blade hummed almost imperceptibly, the vibration traveling up her arm and resonating with something deep within her.

«Together now,» the sword whispered, no longer harsh but seductive in its confidence. «Let me show you what we can accomplish.»

This time, when Koric attacked, Lyra moved with fluid precision. The sword seemed to anticipate each strike, guiding her body with subtle physical cues rather than verbal instructions. Block, parry, sidestep—the sequence emerged naturally, as if she'd practiced it for years.

Blackwood watched with narrowed eyes, her hand resting casually on the hilt of her own weapon. Around them, crew members had paused in their duties to observe the display, murmuring among themselves.

«Now, strike!» the sword commanded as Koric momentarily overextended. «Disable him permanently.»

The bloodthirsty intent behind the command shocked Lyra back to full awareness. Her body, which had been moving in perfect harmony with the blade's guidance, suddenly faltered.

*No!* she rejected the command with every fiber of her being. *This is training, not combat!*

The sword's rage crashed through their connection like a physical blow. «Weakness! Always weakness with you humans!»

Pain exploded behind Lyra's eyes as the sword attempted to force compliance. Her vision blurred, her grip on the hilt loosening. She staggered back, away from Koric, away from the urge to maim that wasn't hers but somehow resided within her.

"Stop," she gasped, unsure whether she was addressing Koric or the sword itself.

Blackwood raised her hand, and Koric immediately lowered his weapon. The captain's expression held genuine curiosity now—and something else. Recognition.

"You're fighting it," she observed. "Interesting. Most new wielders surrender to the power almost immediately."

Lyra stood trembling, the sword still clutched in her hand but held away from her body as if it might bite. In her mind, the blade's fury continued to batter against her resistance.

«You embarrass us both,» it seethed. «That opponent deserved incapacitation at minimum.»

*He's not an enemy*, Lyra insisted. *This is practice, and I won't hurt someone unnecessarily.*

"The conflict is written all over your face," Blackwood commented, circling Lyra with clinical interest. "The Sword of Chaos has always been the most aggressive of the artifacts, demanding violence from its wielders as proof of worthiness."

She stopped directly before Lyra, her scarred face impossible to read. "What does it want you to do right now?"

Lyra swallowed hard. "Kill. Always kill."

Blackwood nodded as if this confirmed a theory. "And yet you resist. Most interesting."

A new voice interrupted from nearby. "Captain, approaching vessel off the starboard bow. Merchant colors, but moving suspiciously fast."

The distraction broke the tension of the moment. Blackwood turned away, immediately shifting into her role as commander. "Battle stations," she ordered. "Prepare to intercept."

The deck erupted into organized chaos as crew members rushed to their positions. Koric moved to follow, but Blackwood held him back with a gesture.

"Take her below," she instructed. "Lock the artifact away, but not too far from her. I want to observe how distance affects their connection."

Lyra felt a surge of panic at the thought of separation from the physical sword, even though its consciousness would remain with her. The blade evidently shared this concern, its anger transmuting to anxiety.

«Resist,» it commanded. «We must remain together.»

But Lyra was too exhausted from their mental battle to put up much physical resistance as Koric led her firmly toward the hatch. As they descended below decks, she heard the rising clamor of a ship preparing for combat—orders shouted, weapons distributed, strategies discussed.

"In here," Koric directed, opening the door to what appeared to be an officer's cabin, smaller than Blackwood's but still private. "Stay until called for."

Lyra entered without protest, but when he reached to take the sword from her hand, she instinctively tightened her grip.

«Do not relinquish me,» the sword demanded. «Fight!»

Koric's expression hardened. "Don't make this difficult, girl. Captain's orders."

They stood at impasse for a moment, tension building between them. Then, with deliberate calm, Lyra loosened her fingers from the hilt.

"Take it," she said quietly, forcing the words past the sword's howl of protest in her mind.

Koric accepted the blade carefully, his movements suggesting he understood its value if not its true nature. "The chest in the corner is prepared for it," he explained, surprisingly patient despite the battle preparations continuing above. "Captain says it needs to be in your vicinity, but not in your possession. Testing your bond, though I don't pretend to understand how."

He placed the sword in an ornate box similar to the one it had been brought in, then closed the lid. Immediately, Lyra felt the connection thin—not breaking, but stretching like a thread pulled taut. The sword's voice became fainter, though no less insistent.

«This separation is unnecessary torture,» it complained. «We function optimally in direct contact.»

*Maybe I function better with some distance between us*, Lyra thought, though without real conviction. Already she could feel a phantom ache developing, centered in her chest where no wound existed.

"You'll remain here during the engagement," Koric informed her, moving toward the door. "Food will be brought later. Don't attempt to leave—the door will be locked, and the crew has orders."

He paused at the threshold, studying her with an expression that might have been pity. "The captain has collected many strange things in her voyages, but you might be the strangest. For your sake, I hope the sword deems you worthy." A shadow crossed his face. "I've seen what happens to those it rejects."

Before Lyra could ask what he meant, the door closed and the lock engaged with a definitive click.

Left alone with the sword's muted presence, Lyra moved to the small porthole. Outside, the *Tempest* was closing on what appeared to be a merchant vessel flying the colors of the Eastern Kingdoms. Even from this distance, she could see the panic on the other ship as it attempted to outrun its pursuer—a futile effort against Blackwood's legendary vessel.

«You watch from a cage while blood is spilled without you,» the sword observed, its voice fainter but still clear enough. «This is not our purpose.»

*Our purpose?* Lyra questioned. *You said before that you were created for something other than violence. What was it?*

The sword's presence hesitated, an unusual pause in its normally confident assertions. «Fragments remain. Memories buried beneath centuries of...use. I was forged to protect boundaries, to sever corrupted connections.»

*Not to kill?*

«Killing was incidental, not central.» Another pause. «This conversation depletes energy we cannot afford to waste while separated.»

Lyra sensed truth in this. Already the phantom ache in her chest had intensified, a persistent discomfort that made it difficult to focus on anything else. She moved to the bunk and sat, trying to regulate her breathing as the sounds of battle began above—shouts, running feet, the distant crack of what might have been cannon fire.

Minutes stretched into hours. The battle sounds gradually subsided, replaced by the organized chaos of aftermath—the cries of the wounded, commands being issued, the creak of ropes as captured cargo was transferred. Through it all, Lyra remained locked away, the sword's presence a constant, if diminished, companion in her mind.

When the door finally opened, it was Nari who entered, carrying a tray of food. Her appearance surprised Lyra, who had expected a guard or perhaps Blackwood herself.

"You're allowed to bring me food?" she asked as Nari set the tray on the small chest.

The girl glanced back at the open doorway where a guard stood watching. "I volunteered. Blackwood doesn't care who delivers your meals as long as you're monitored." She lowered her voice. "Don't react to what I'm about to say."

Lyra maintained a neutral expression with effort, acutely aware of the guard's eyes on them.

"Blackwood's obsession with the artifacts is dangerous," Nari murmured, arranging items on the tray with deliberate slowness. "They're changing her. The crew's noticed, though no one dares speak of it. Mood swings. Increasing recklessness. Talking to herself just like you do."

«The artifacts corrupt those not strong enough to master them,» the sword commented. «Your Captain Blackwood handles power like a child with fire—fascinated by the flames while ignoring the spreading smoke.»

Lyra silently relayed this observation to Nari, who nodded almost imperceptibly.

"She's planning something with you and that sword," Nari continued, still pretending to arrange the food. "Something to do with the next artifact she's hunting."

"The Root of Stone," Lyra said quietly.

Nari's eyes widened slightly. "Yes. How did you—"

"She told me. She has the other two already."

"Which is two too many," Nari replied grimly. "One more minute," she called to the guard before turning back to Lyra. "Listen, if you get a chance to escape, take it. Don't wait for me or anyone else. The artifacts are poison."

«Yet here you are, bound to one,» the sword interjected with something like amusement.

*Not by choice*, Lyra retorted.

«Few worthy vessels choose their burden.»

The guard cleared his throat impatiently, and Nari straightened. "Eat while it's warm," she said at normal volume. "Better than the slop they serve the regular prisoners."

As she turned to leave, Lyra caught her wrist. "Be careful," she whispered, surprising herself with the genuine concern she felt for this girl she barely knew.

Nari's expression softened momentarily before her mask of casual indifference returned. "Always am," she replied, then departed with the guard, leaving Lyra alone once more.

The food was indeed better than what she'd had in the hold—fresh bread, some kind of fish stew, even a small cup of watered wine. Lyra ate mechanically, her appetite diminished by the constant discomfort of separation from the sword's physical form.

«Proximity matters,» the sword explained as if sensing her thoughts. «The blood bond connects us regardless of distance, but physical closeness strengthens our communication and abilities.»

*Is that why you're showing me how to fight? To make me a better weapon?*

The sword's response held an unexpected note of complexity. «I show you how to fight because it is what I know. It is what I have become.»

Before Lyra could pursue this cryptic statement, the door opened again. This time it was Captain Blackwood who entered, still wearing the practical clothing from their training session but now with blood spattering one sleeve—evidence of the recent battle.

"Your bond appears stable even with separation," she observed without preamble. "Most interesting."

She crossed to the chest where the sword lay and opened it. Immediately, Lyra felt the connection strengthen, the phantom ache in her chest easing. The sword's presence in her mind sharpened, its satisfaction evident.

«Reunited,» it purred. «Though not yet in hand where we belong.»

Blackwood studied Lyra's face. "You feel it, don't you? The relief when it's nearer. That dependency will only grow stronger with time."

"Is that what happened to you?" Lyra asked, gesturing toward the captain's bloodstained sleeve. "Dependency on magical objects that make you kill more efficiently?"

Instead of the anger Lyra expected, Blackwood smiled—a genuine expression that transformed her scarred face into something almost beautiful. "Oh, I killed efficiently long before I found the artifacts. The difference is now I kill with purpose."

She lifted the sword from its chest, holding it with the casual confidence of someone accustomed to weapons. Lyra felt a jolt of... something... through their connection. Not quite jealousy, but a distinct discomfort at seeing her bonded artifact in another's hands.

"Let me tell you what happens next," Blackwood said, examining the blade as she spoke. "Tomorrow we reach the Northern Archipelago. There, according to my sources, we'll find a tribal warrior worthy of bonding with the Root of Stone. Once I possess all four artifacts, I'll perform a ritual to unite them under my control."

"And what happens to me?" Lyra asked, though she suspected she already knew the answer.

Blackwood returned the sword to its chest, closing the lid with deliberate care. "That depends on you. If you cooperate—help me understand the sword's abilities, train with it as I direct—there might be a place for you in what comes after."

"And if I don't cooperate?"

The captain's expression hardened. "Then I'll find another way to separate you from the blade. Less pleasant for you, certainly, but ultimately the same result." She moved toward the door. "Consider your options carefully, Lyra of Stillwater. Your village is gone. Your family is gone. All you have now is what I choose to give you."

The words struck with calculated precision, targeting the grief Lyra had barely allowed herself to acknowledge. As the door closed behind Blackwood, tears welled up unbidden, the first she'd shed since witnessing Merina's death.

«Grief is a luxury we cannot afford,» the sword stated, though its tone lacked its usual harshness. «The pirate queen is right about one thing—you must decide what role you will play in this unfolding game.»

*What game?* Lyra thought bitterly, wiping away tears. *I'm a prisoner with no choices. Bound to a bloodthirsty weapon and held captive by a power-mad pirate.*

«All games have rules,» the sword continued. «Rules can be exploited. The woman who calls herself Blackwood knows much about artifacts but understands little of their true nature. This is to our advantage.»

*Our?* Lyra questioned. *You seem more interested in getting me to kill for you than in helping me survive.*

The sword's response was unexpectedly measured. «Your survival and my purpose are not as opposed as you believe. I require a wielder. You require power to escape your circumstances. We need not agree on how that power is used to benefit from our alliance.»

*That's not an alliance. That's a compromise.*

«Then compromise,» the sword urged. «Train with me. Learn what I can teach you. When opportunity presents itself, we will decide together how to proceed.»

Lyra considered this proposal carefully. The sword offered knowledge and power she desperately needed, even if its ultimate goals remained suspect. Blackwood offered only the certainty of eventual separation—likely through her death.

*I'll train with you*, she decided finally. *But I won't kill unnecessarily. And I won't surrender my will to yours.*

«A beginning,» the sword acknowledged. «Though you will find your definition of 'necessary' changing with experience.»

Exhausted by the day's events, Lyra stretched out on the narrow bunk. Outside the small porthole, stars had appeared, pinpricks of light in the vast darkness. Somewhere beneath them, the sea carried the *Tempest* ever northward, toward whatever fate awaited at the Northern Archipelago.

As sleep claimed her, Lyra found herself wondering which was the greater threat—the captain who collected artifacts like trophies, or the sword that had claimed her blood without consent. Between these two forces, was there any path that led to freedom?

The sword, still conscious as she drifted into dreams, offered no answers—only the certainty that tomorrow would bring another test of wills, another step toward whatever she was becoming.
