# Chapter 2: Blood and Blade

The storm arrived three days after Merrick's warning.

Lyra woke to the distant boom of thunder, different from the familiar rhythm of waves against Stillwater's shore. She sat up in her narrow bed, the lapis lazuli pendant cool against her skin despite the humid night air. Outside her window, the moon struggled against encroaching clouds, casting fitful shadows across the floor.

*Something's wrong*. The thought came unbidden, accompanied by a prickling sensation at the base of her neck.

She slipped from beneath her thin blanket and padded to the window. The village lay quiet below, thatched roofs silvered by moonlight, but beyond—out where sea met sky—pinpricks of light dotted the darkness. Not stars. Not fishing lanterns either; the fleet wouldn't venture out with a storm approaching.

Her throat tightened. Ships. Too many ships.

A sharp rap at the front door startled her. Voices murmured below—her father's sleep-rough grunt, then another voice, urgent and breathless. Lyra pulled a shawl around her shoulders and crept to the top of the narrow stairs.

"...Blackwood's fleet," the village watchman was saying, his words sending ice through Lyra's veins. "Six ships at least, approaching fast. Elder Gareth says to gather only what you can carry."

"And go where?" her father demanded. "There's nowhere to hide an entire village."

"The caves beyond the northern point. Merina says—"

"Merina says, Merina says," her father interrupted. "I trust the sea over an old woman's feelings."

Lyra's mother appeared from the bedroom, already dressed. "Thom," she said quietly. "Wake the girls."

Something in her mother's tone silenced further argument. Her father nodded tersely, and the watchman hurried away to the next house.

Events blurred into frantic motion. Her younger sisters, bleary-eyed and confused, were bundled into warm clothes. Her mother packed a small bag with medicines and dried foods. All the while, Lyra's hands moved automatically, helping, packing, but her mind remained fixed on the lights approaching from the sea.

"Lyra." Her mother's voice broke through her daze. "Go to Merina. She'll need help with the village records."

The order surprised her. "But shouldn't we stay together?"

Her mother's face softened momentarily. "The old woman has been good to you. And she'll need strong arms to carry her scrolls." She pressed something into Lyra's palm—a small knife in a leather sheath. "Go quickly."

Outside, the village was awakening to fear. Lanterns bobbed as families emerged from their homes, children clutching prized possessions, parents carrying hastily packed bundles. The orderly evacuation Lyra had expected fragmented as the first distant crack of cannon fire echoed across the water.

Panic rippled through the gathering. Some fled immediately toward the northern path, while others rushed to the docks, determined to launch boats. Lyra pushed against the current of bodies, making for Merina's isolated hut.

The wind had risen, carrying the scent of rain and something sharper—gunpowder. Another cannon blast, closer now. Lyra broke into a run, her bare feet slipping on the dewy path. The wind chimes outside Merina's hut jangled frenetically, their usual musical tones transformed into frantic warnings.

"Merina!" she called, pushing through the doorway without her customary pause.

The old woman knelt before a small chest, placing scrolls and bound records inside. She looked up, unsurprised by Lyra's appearance. "Good. You're here. Take this." She thrust the chest toward Lyra.

"We need to go," Lyra urged, taking the surprisingly heavy container. "The pirates—"

"Are already here," Merina finished. Her calm was unnerving. "There's no time to reach the caves. We must hide these records."

"But—"

The old woman's hands gripped Lyra's shoulders with unexpected strength. "Listen carefully, child. The Stillwater records contain information Captain Blackwood must not find. Take them to the hollow oak at the edge of the marsh. Hide them and yourself there."

"What about you?"

Merina's smile was serene, mismatched against the growing chaos outside. "My path ends here, as I've seen for many nights now."

"No!" The denial burst from Lyra, sudden and fierce. "Come with me. Please."

A scream cut through the night, followed by the unmistakable crack of splintering wood. The first pirates had reached the village.

"Go," Merina commanded, pressing something else into Lyra's hands—a small leather pouch. "When you need answers, look to water and flame together."

Before Lyra could protest further, the old woman pushed her toward the back of the hut, where a small door led out to the herb garden. "Remember your dreams, Lyra Nightwind. They weren't warnings—they were preparation."

The sky beyond had transformed, orange flames now visible in the village below. The air filled with shouts, screams, and the thunder of approaching boots. Lyra clutched the chest to her body, paralyzed between flight and loyalty.

"GO!" Merina's command broke her stillness.

Heart pounding in her ears, Lyra ran. Away from the hut, away from the village, toward the dark silhouette of the marsh oak. Behind her, wood cracked and men shouted. She didn't look back—couldn't look back—even as Merina's wind chimes gave one final, discordant jangle before falling silent.

The marsh oak loomed ahead, ancient and gnarled. Its massive trunk, hollowed by lightning strike years before, created a natural hiding place. Lyra squeezed through the opening, scraping her shoulders, and set the chest down in the darkness. She pressed herself as far back as possible, drawing her knees to her chest.

Through the narrow opening, Stillwater burned.

Orange flames leapt from thatched roofs, casting monstrous shadows against the night. Dark figures moved through the village—some running, some pursuing. The screams had resolved into individual voices she recognized. Elder Gareth bellowing orders. The blacksmith's daughter calling for her father. A child wailing for its mother.

*I should be there. I should be helping.* The thought twisted in her gut like a knife.

But her body refused to move, frozen by a fear more primal than shame. She watched, silent and trembling, as pirates dragged villagers from hiding places. Through gaps in the trees, she could see figures being herded toward the docks like cattle.

A larger silhouette appeared at the edge of the village—a woman in a long coat, her hair elaborately braided. Even from this distance, her commanding presence was unmistakable. Captain Blackwood had arrived personally to oversee the raid.

The pirate captain surveyed the burning village with casual interest, occasionally giving orders to those around her. When Elder Gareth was brought before her, struggling between two burly pirates, she questioned him briefly before making a dismissive gesture. The elder was added to the group of captives rather than cut down. Blackwood wanted prisoners, not just destruction.

"Please be safe," Lyra whispered, thinking of her parents and sisters. Guilt churned inside her. She should have stayed with them. She should have insisted Merina come with her. She should have—

Movement near the oracle's hut caught her attention. Flames now engulfed the small structure, but in their orange light, Lyra could see Merina being escorted—no, dragged—toward Captain Blackwood. Unlike the other villagers, the old woman walked with dignity despite rough handling.

Blackwood stepped forward to meet the oracle, saying something Lyra couldn't hear. Merina's response seemed to anger the captain. She gestured sharply, and one of her officers brought forward a scroll—a map or drawing of some kind. Again, Merina spoke, her face calm even as Blackwood's expression darkened with rage.

What happened next burned itself into Lyra's memory forever.

Captain Blackwood drew her sword—a fluid, practiced motion—and thrust it through Merina's chest in a single movement. The old woman's body jerked, then crumpled to the ground. The captain wiped her blade clean on the oracle's clothing before turning away, already issuing new orders to her crew.

Lyra bit into her fist to keep from screaming. The metallic taste of blood filled her mouth as her teeth broke skin. Tears streamed down her face, but she didn't dare make a sound. Not when pirates were still searching the area, torches illuminating their hunt for more villagers.

One such torch came terrifyingly close to the marsh oak. Lyra pressed further back into the hollow, barely breathing as boots crunched on fallen leaves just outside. The torch's light flickered through the opening, sending shadows dancing across her hiding place.

"Nothing here but an old tree," a gruff voice said.

"Keep looking," another responded. "Captain wants them all."

The torch moved away, and Lyra released her breath in a silent, shuddering exhale. Hours passed this way—crouched in darkness, listening to the destruction of everything she had ever known. The sounds gradually shifted from violence to organized activity. Commands being issued. Supplies being loaded. The creak of wood as ships prepared to depart.

Dawn's gray light filtered through the opening when Lyra finally dared to move. Her limbs had stiffened painfully, and every muscle protested as she edged toward the hollow's entrance. The village below lay in ruins. Smoke curled from collapsed buildings. The docks, once crowded with fishing boats, stood empty save for splintered remains.

Most of the pirate ships had departed, their sails now distant smudges on the horizon. One vessel remained, sleek and menacing with crimson sails—the flagship. Figures moved along the shore, gathering final supplies and stragglers.

Lyra knew she should stay hidden. Wait until they were truly gone. But a desperate need to know her family's fate pushed her forward. Clutching Merina's pouch and her mother's knife, she left the chest hidden in the hollow and began a cautious descent toward what remained of Stillwater.

The village was unrecognizable. Charred timbers had replaced familiar homes. The communal ovens lay shattered. Body shapes beneath hastily placed covers marked those who had resisted too fiercely or simply been unlucky. Lyra moved from body to body, dreading what she might find yet unable to stop searching.

She had nearly reached the docks when a hand clamped over her mouth from behind. She tried to scream, to bite, to use the knife still clutched in her fist, but her attacker was too strong.

"Quiet, girl," a man's voice hissed in her ear. "Unless you want to join the dead."

The pirate's breath was hot against her neck, smelling of rum and rotted teeth. He dragged her toward the waiting ship, where the last boats were being loaded with captives. Through tear-blurred eyes, Lyra saw familiar faces among the prisoners—the blacksmith's family, several fishermen, children from neighboring houses.

No sign of her parents or sisters.

"Got another one, Captain!" her captor called proudly as they approached the loading area. "Hiding up near the woods!"

Captain Blackwood turned, and Lyra found herself face-to-face with the woman who had destroyed her world. The pirate queen was tall and lean, her black hair intricately braided with gold threads that caught the morning light. A distinctive scar ran from her left temple to jaw. Her crimson coat, edged with gold braid, hung open to reveal practical leather armor beneath.

But it was her eyes that held Lyra captive—cold, calculating, and utterly without mercy.

"Where did you find this one?" Blackwood asked, her voice surprisingly melodic for someone who commanded such violence.

"Skulking near the ruins, Captain."

Blackwood's gaze swept over Lyra, assessing. "Search her."

Rough hands emptied her pockets, confiscated her mother's knife, and tore Merina's pouch from her grasp. Lyra barely registered these violations; she was lost in the growing realization that her entire world had ended overnight.

The pirate handed Merina's pouch to Blackwood, who loosened the drawstring and emptied the contents into her palm. A small object fell out—a carved stone that glinted blue in the morning light. Lyra recognized it as one of the oracle's treasured divination stones.

Something flickered in Blackwood's expression—recognition, perhaps even triumph. She closed her fingers around the stone and addressed Lyra directly for the first time.

"You were with the oracle," she stated rather than asked. "What did she tell you about the artifact?"

Lyra's mind raced. Artifact? What artifact? She thought of Merina's final words: *When you need answers, look to water and flame together.*

"I don't know what you mean," she managed, her voice sounding strange to her own ears.

Blackwood studied her for a long moment, then smiled thinly. "Take her with the others. She may prove useful later."

Lyra was half-dragged, half-carried to the waiting boat. As they shoved her down beside other captives, she caught a last glimpse of Stillwater—smoking ruins where her life had once been. Merina's body lay where she had fallen, now just one among many shrouded forms scattered throughout the village.

The boat pushed off from shore, its wake spreading ripples across the blood-tinged water. No one spoke. Most captives stared blankly, shock rendering them mute. A child whimpered until its mother pulled it close, murmuring empty reassurances.

Above them loomed the pirate flagship, the *Tempest*, its crimson sails catching the morning breeze. Lyra felt a strange detachment as she was hauled aboard, her body responding automatically while her mind retreated. Someone bound her wrists with rough rope. Someone else shoved her below decks with the other prisoners. Words were spoken—threats, instructions—but they washed over her without meaning.

As the ship moved away from the shore, Lyra sat in the cramped darkness of the hold, surrounded by the remnants of her village. The numbness that had protected her began to crack, allowing grief to seep through. She bit her lip again to keep from making a sound, tasting fresh blood.

The rocking motion of the ship seemed to mock the stability she had taken for granted her whole life. Everything familiar—gone. Everyone she loved—gone or captured.

And she, who had dreamed of seeing beyond Stillwater's horizon, was now being carried into that unknown—not as an adventurer but as a prisoner. The bitter irony was not lost on her, even through the haze of shock.

The *Tempest* cut through the waves, bearing Lyra and the other survivors away from the smoking ruins of their former lives. Behind them, the rising sun painted the sky in shades of blood and fire, a final echo of the night's destruction.

Somewhere deep in her mind, water and flame mingled once again. Not in dreams this time, but in the harsh reality of what her life had become.
