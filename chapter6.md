# Chapter 6: Unexpected Allies

The Northern Archipelago appeared as a jagged silhouette against the dawn sky, its mountains like teeth biting into the fading darkness. Lyra stood at the railing of the *Tempest*, watching as the distant islands slowly took form. She'd been permitted topside more frequently since her training began—another subtle shift in her status from prisoner to something not quite defined.

The sword rested against her back in its sheath, a constant weight both physical and mental. After days of training with Blackwood, their bond had settled into an uneasy rhythm. The blade no longer felt like an intruder in her mind so much as an unwelcome roommate—still foreign, but familiar in its foreignness.

«The northern tribes will resist Blackwood's intrusion,» the sword observed, its thoughts flowing into Lyra's with practiced ease. «They've guarded their sovereignty for centuries. This will not be a peaceful encounter.»

*Is that anticipation I hear?* Lyra thought back, having mastered their silent communication.

«Merely observation. Though I won't deny combat would provide valuable training opportunities.»

A week ago, such bloodthirsty commentary would have disturbed her. Now she simply acknowledged it with resigned familiarity. The sword's nature hadn't changed, but her reaction to it had—adaptation, not acceptance.

Below on the main deck, the day's work routine had begun. Lyra watched as the first group of captives was led from the hold—familiar faces from Stillwater, thinner now, their movements sluggish from poor food and confined quarters. Behind them came prisoners taken from other raids, distinguished by their different clothing and dialects. Last emerged a cluster of children, pale from lack of sunlight, huddled together for protection.

The sight stirred something in Lyra that she'd been too numb to feel during her own initial captivity—indignation bordering on anger.

«Your pulse quickens,» the sword noted with interest. «Anger serves us well in combat.»

*This isn't about combat*, Lyra responded. *Those children are being treated like cargo.*

Without fully forming a plan, she descended from her position and approached the group. The guard—a brawny woman with a perpetual scowl—eyed her suspiciously but didn't interfere. Lyra's strange status afforded her a degree of freedom that both guards and captives recognized without understanding.

She reached the youngest children first, a boy and girl who couldn't be more than seven or eight years old. Both stared at her with vacant expressions that spoke of trauma and resignation far beyond their years.

"When did you last eat properly?" Lyra asked softly.

The boy looked at his feet. The girl whispered, "Yesterday morning."

Lyra's jaw tightened. She straightened and turned to the guard. "These children need more food. They can't work effectively if they're starving."

The guard's expression shifted from surprise to amusement. "And I suppose you're going to feed them yourself?"

"If necessary," Lyra replied, meeting the woman's gaze without flinching. Something in her tone or stance must have communicated more than her words, because the guard's amusement faltered.

«She fears what I might do through you,» the sword commented smugly. «Your connection to me gives your words weight.»

"I'll speak to the quartermaster," the guard said after a moment, her gruff tone belying the concession. "No promises."

It was a small victory, barely worth noting, but the children's faces showed the first flicker of something other than fear. Lyra felt a strange sensation unfurl in her chest—not the sword's presence, but something she'd nearly forgotten: purpose.

As the morning progressed, Lyra remained with the captives rather than reporting to her regular training. No one questioned her—another benefit of her ambiguous position. She helped distribute water, supported an elderly man who struggled with his assigned task of coiling ropes, and intervened when a particularly aggressive crewman seemed ready to strike a woman for working too slowly.

The sword observed all of this with a mixture of confusion and reluctant interest. «You spend your energy on these weaklings rather than improving your own skills. I don't understand the strategy.»

*Not everything is about strategy*, Lyra replied, helping a young boy untangle a knot in the fishing lines he'd been assigned to sort. *Sometimes it's just about doing what's right.*

«Yet you gain something,» the sword insisted, clearly struggling to categorize her behavior within its framework of power and advantage. «The captives look to you now, defer to you. You've established dominance without violence.»

Lyra almost laughed aloud at this interpretation. *I haven't established dominance. I've shown kindness. There's a difference.*

«Is there?» The sword's question seemed genuinely puzzled. «Both result in others following your lead.»

Before Lyra could formulate a response, a commotion erupted near the forecastle. A younger crew member was berating one of the captives—a girl perhaps fourteen years old with sunburned shoulders and tear-streaked cheeks. The girl had apparently spilled water meant for washing the deck, and the crewman's face was flushed with disproportionate rage.

"Worthless island trash!" he snarled, raising his hand to strike her.

Lyra moved without thinking, crossing the distance in rapid strides. She caught the man's wrist before his blow could land, her grip firm.

"Enough," she said, her voice low but carrying. "She'll clean it up. There's no need for this."

The crewman's eyes widened, first in shock that anyone would interfere, then in recognition of who had stopped him. His gaze flicked to the sword visible over her shoulder, and Lyra felt the sword's satisfaction at the fear that flickered across his face.

«He knows what I could do to him,» the sword purred. «Let me show him. Just a small demonstration.»

*No*, Lyra replied firmly. *This ends without bloodshed.*

"The captain's pet," the crewman sneered, masking his unease with bravado as he yanked his arm free. "Playing protector now, are we? These bilge rats aren't worth your concern."

From the corner of her eye, Lyra noticed Koric watching from nearby, his expression unreadable. Whether he would intervene on her behalf or against her remained uncertain, but his presence added weight to the confrontation.

"Captain Blackwood values efficiency," Lyra said, keeping her voice steady despite the sword's eagerness humming through their connection. "Beating workers reduces their efficiency. Simple logic."

It was the kind of reasoning the sword understood and approved of, even if it wasn't Lyra's true motivation. The appeal to pragmatism also seemed to register with the crewman, who clearly didn't want to appear irrational in front of his superiors.

"Fine," he muttered, stepping back. "But she better work twice as hard to make up for it."

He stalked away, dignity somewhat intact. The girl stared at Lyra with a mixture of gratitude and bewilderment, as if unable to comprehend why anyone would defend her.

"Thank you," she whispered.

"Get some fresh water and clean this up quickly," Lyra advised. "Don't give them reasons to single you out."

As the girl hurried to comply, Lyra became aware of the silence that had fallen over the deck. Captives and crew alike had paused to observe the confrontation, and the shift in their gazes told her something had changed. The captives looked at her with cautious hope; the crew with new calculation.

«You've altered your position in the hierarchy,» the sword observed. «Fascinating. Without drawing me, without spilling blood, you've established yourself as someone of consequence.»

*I'm just trying to help people survive*, Lyra responded, uncomfortable with the attention.

«Intention aside, the effect is the same.» The sword's tone suggested it was reassessing its understanding of power dynamics. «They respect you. Or fear you. The distinction may be irrelevant.»

Koric approached, his expression now openly curious. "The captain wants to see you," he said, loud enough for those nearby to hear. Then, more quietly: "That was either very brave or very foolish."

"Which would you prefer it to be?" Lyra asked.

A ghost of a smile crossed his face. "Brave makes a better story, but foolish is often more honest." He gestured toward the captain's quarters. "Don't keep her waiting."

As Lyra followed him across the deck, she felt eyes tracking her movement—both hostile and hopeful. Whatever nebulous status she'd held before had crystalized into something more defined. The sword was right; she had changed her position. The implications of that change remained to be seen.

Captain Blackwood was not alone in her quarters. Beside her stood Vex, the pale guardian of the Chalice of Tides, her expression as cool and unreadable as ever. The chalice itself rested on the desk between them, its silver surface liquid-smooth and somehow menacing despite its beauty.

"Ah, my unexpected champion of the unfortunate," Blackwood greeted Lyra, her tone hovering between amusement and irritation. "Word travels quickly on a ship. You've been busy this morning."

Lyra didn't respond, sensing a trap in Blackwood's seemingly casual opening.

"I wonder," the captain continued, circling the desk to stand directly before Lyra, "is this newfound compassion your own trait, or has the sword's influence awakened something in you?"

«She probes for weakness,» the sword cautioned. «Reveal nothing.»

"Does it matter?" Lyra asked. "The captives work better when they're not being abused. Even pirates should understand basic efficiency."

Blackwood laughed, the sound surprisingly genuine. "Oh, she's learning," she said to Vex, who remained impassive. "Using logic rather than moral arguments. Perhaps there's hope for her after all."

She returned to her position behind the desk, her amusement fading into calculated assessment. "Still, I didn't bring you here to discuss your humanitarian efforts. We reach the Northern Archipelago by nightfall, and there are preparations to make."

"What kind of preparations?" Lyra asked, conscious of the sword's heightened attention.

"The stone-shapers are not naturally welcoming to outsiders," Blackwood explained. "But they respect power, particularly power connected to the ancient artifacts." She gestured to Vex. "Show her."

Vex lifted the chalice, her movements fluid and precise. The metal began to ripple more actively, then rose in a thin silver stream that defied gravity, coiling through the air like a living thing. It formed shapes—first a perfect sphere, then a complex geometric pattern, finally a miniature likeness of the *Tempest* itself—before returning to the chalice with a musical note that resonated in Lyra's bones.

"Impressive control," Blackwood commented. "The chalice responds to Vex because they share an affinity for order and structure." She turned her attention back to Lyra. "The sword chose you for qualities I'm still discerning. Together with Ilaria and the Feather, we present a unified front that the northern tribes will recognize as significant."

«She parades us like exotic animals,» the sword said with disdain. «Yet she understands little of our true nature.»

Lyra silently agreed, though she kept her expression neutral. "What exactly do you expect me to do?"

"Simply be present," Blackwood replied. "Let the sword be visible. When called upon, demonstrate its power—but only as I direct." Her gaze hardened. "This is delicate diplomacy, not a raid. I want the Root of Stone and its potential wielder acquired willingly, not through force."

"And if I refuse to participate?"

The question hung in the air, bold in its directness. Vex's eyes widened slightly, but Blackwood merely smiled.

"You won't," she said with absolute certainty. "Your behavior this morning confirms what I've suspected—you're developing investment in the welfare of your fellow captives. Their continued well-being depends entirely on my goodwill."

The threat, though implicit, was unmistakable. Lyra's jaw tightened, but she remained silent, acknowledging the trap she'd walked into by showing concern for others.

"Dismissed," Blackwood said, already turning her attention to maps spread across the desk. "Prepare yourself. I expect you on deck at sunset, fully armed and composed."

Outside, the day had brightened, sunlight sparkling off waves that grew steadily choppier as they approached the archipelago's natural wind currents. Lyra paused at the railing, trying to process the implications of this new role Blackwood had cast her in.

«She manipulates skillfully,» the sword acknowledged with what sounded almost like respect. «Using your compassion as a lever against you.»

*She misunderstands compassion if she thinks it makes me weaker*, Lyra replied, watching a flock of unfamiliar seabirds wheel above the approaching islands.

«Perhaps. Or perhaps she understands that attachment creates vulnerability.» The sword's tone shifted, becoming unexpectedly reflective. «Previous wielders who formed attachments often became more erratic, less effective in combat.»

*Is that why you discourage connections? To make me a better weapon?*

The sword's response came slowly, as if it were considering ideas it hadn't examined before. «Efficiency in combat requires singular focus. Attachments divide attention. This has always seemed self-evident.»

*Yet you're attached to me now*, Lyra pointed out. *Our bond is a form of attachment, isn't it?*

The sword's confusion rippled through their connection. «This is different. The blood bond serves purpose. It enhances ability. Your human connections serve no tactical advantage.»

"You look troubled."

The voice, spoken aloud behind her, jolted Lyra from her internal dialogue. She turned to find Nari watching her with those sharp, observant eyes. The younger girl's appearance had improved since their first meeting; like Lyra, she seemed to occupy a precarious position between captive and crew, allowed certain freedoms while never forgetting her status.

"Just thinking," Lyra replied.

Nari glanced around to ensure no one was within earshot before stepping closer to the railing. "I heard what you did this morning. Impressive, standing up to Derrick like that. He's got a reputation for cruelty."

"It wasn't planned," Lyra admitted. "I just couldn't watch anymore."

"Planned or not, it's changed things." Nari's expression was serious beyond her years. "The captives see you as some kind of protector now. The crew isn't sure what to make of you."

«She states the obvious, but her assessment matches mine,» the sword commented. «This girl has uncommon perception.»

"Blackwood will use that to her advantage," Nari continued, confirming the sword's observation. "She excels at turning every situation to serve her goals."

Lyra studied the younger girl, noting the intelligence in her eyes and the careful way she held herself—always aware, always calculating. "You know her well."

"Better than most." Nari's gaze drifted toward the captain's quarters. "Three years watching someone teaches you their patterns."

"You said before that your father was Captain Kerrigan," Lyra prompted gently. "That Blackwood killed him."

For a moment, Nari's carefully maintained composure wavered, revealing a flash of raw grief that time had failed to dull. "The *Seaflower* was my home for fifteen years. My father taught me navigation, tactics, how to read wind patterns and predict storms." Her voice remained steady despite the emotion in her eyes. "We were privateers, not pirates. We operated with letters of marque from the Eastern Kingdoms."

"What happened?" Lyra asked, sensing Nari needed to speak of this almost as much as she needed to hear it.

"Blackwood happened." Nari's hands tightened on the railing. "The *Tempest* intercepted us returning from a sanctioned mission. We were damaged from a previous engagement, low on supplies. My father signaled surrender, expecting quarter as we would have given." Her expression hardened. "Blackwood executed him personally, in front of the entire crew. Then she offered them a choice—join her fleet or join him."

«Honor among pirates is as substantial as mist on water,» the sword commented. «This surprises you?»

*Cruelty always surprises me*, Lyra responded. *And should.*

"Why did she keep you alive?" she asked Nari.

"Insurance. My father had allies among other privateer captains and within the Eastern Kingdoms' naval command. My continued survival keeps them from pursuing vengeance too aggressively." Nari's smile was bitter. "I'm more valuable as a hostage than as a corpse."

The conversation paused as a group of crew members passed nearby, eyeing them suspiciously but not interfering. When they had moved on, Nari continued in a lower voice.

"Blackwood wasn't always like this. In my first year aboard, she was calculating but not cruel. Something changed when she found the chalice. She became... obsessed. The feather accelerated the transformation." She glanced meaningfully at the sword on Lyra's back. "The artifacts change their wielders, whether they realize it or not."

«She speaks with limited understanding,» the sword dismissed, though Lyra detected a defensive quality to its response. «Weak vessels are changed by power. Strong ones master it.»

Before Lyra could ask more, commotion erupted on the far side of the deck. Sailors rushed toward the commotion, their shouts carrying across the ship. Lyra and Nari exchanged glances before moving toward the source of the disturbance.

A child had somehow climbed onto the railing—the same boy Lyra had spoken to that morning, his thin face now contorted with fear. He teetered precariously as the ship pitched, one small hand gripping a loose line that was never intended to bear weight.

"Fell from the rigging," a nearby sailor explained tersely. "Climbed up when no one was watching. Stupid brat."

The boy's position was desperate—suspended over open water, his grip visibly weakening on the rope. The turbulent sea beneath promised certain death for a child who likely couldn't swim, and the ship's movement made reaching him without causing him to fall nearly impossible.

Without conscious decision, Lyra found her hand moving to the sword's hilt. *Can we help him?*

«Combat applications are not suitable for rescue operations,» the sword replied dismissively.

*I don't need combat. I need... a boundary.* The word came to her instinctively, remembering the glowing path the sword had created during their demonstration for Blackwood. *Something to catch him if he falls.*

The sword's interest sharpened. «Boundary definition. Yes, that might be possible. Draw me. Focus on creating a surface beneath him.»

Lyra unsheathed the blade, its familiar weight settling into her palm with practiced ease. The crew members nearby backed away instinctively, creating space as she raised the sword toward the boy.

«Visualize energy extending from the blade,» the sword instructed, its mental voice now focused and instructional rather than dismissive. «See it as a plane, solid enough to support weight but permeable to your will.»

Concentrating, Lyra directed her attention toward the space beneath the terrified child. The sword warmed in her hand, vibrating slightly as energy flowed from her body through the metal. A faint blue-gold shimmer appeared in the air, stretching from the blade's tip toward the boy.

"Hold on!" she called to him. "Don't let go yet!"

The boy's eyes widened at the sight of the magical energy extending beneath him. His grip on the rope slipped further, his small body swaying dangerously with the ship's motion.

«More focus,» the sword urged. «You're hesitating. Commitment forms the boundary.»

Lyra redoubled her concentration, imagining the energy solidifying into a tangible surface. The blue-gold light intensified, spreading wider. She could feel the sword drawing more deeply on her energy, the drain manifesting as a growing heaviness in her limbs.

The moment the boundary fully formed, the boy's grip gave way. He fell with a cry that turned to surprise as he landed on the glowing surface, which gave slightly under his weight but held firm. The spectral platform hovered impossibly between ship and sea, defying natural law.

"Now what?" Lyra murmured, sweat beading on her forehead from the effort of maintaining the boundary.

«Intent directs the energy,» the sword explained. «Will the boundary to move.»

Following this guidance, Lyra concentrated on bringing the boy back to the ship. The glowing surface responded, sliding smoothly through the air until it aligned with the deck. The boy scrambled off, collapsing into the arms of a woman Lyra recognized as another Stillwater captive.

As soon as he was safe, Lyra released the energy. The boundary dissipated into motes of light that faded against the afternoon sun. Exhaustion hit her immediately—a bone-deep weariness that made her sway on her feet. Nari appeared at her side, steadying her with a supportive grip.

"That was..." Nari began, her expression caught between awe and wariness.

"Remarkable," a new voice completed.

Lyra turned to find Captain Blackwood watching from nearby, her scarred face alive with interest. Vex and Ilaria stood behind her, their respective artifacts visible—the chalice rippling in apparent agitation, the feather spinning rapidly between Ilaria's fingers.

"I knew the Sword of Chaos had capabilities beyond mere destruction," Blackwood continued, approaching with measured steps. "But protective magic? That's unexpected from an artifact known for centuries as a dealer of death."

«She sounds impressed, but her surprise concerns me,» the sword said privately to Lyra. «If she believed the artifacts were purely weapons, her plans may be more flawed than we realized.»

The captain circled Lyra slowly, studying her as one might examine an unexpected variant in a familiar species. "You continue to surprise me, Lyra of Stillwater. First your compassion for the weak, now this demonstration of control and restraint."

Her gaze shifted to the sword, still drawn in Lyra's hand. "I wonder... is it you influencing the blade, or has it uncovered qualities in you that I failed to recognize?"

Before Lyra could formulate a response, the exhaustion overcame her. Her knees buckled, and darkness edged her vision. The last thing she saw was Blackwood's calculating expression and the boy she'd saved staring at her with a mixture of fear and wonder.

---

Consciousness returned gradually, like a tide washing in scattered memories. Lyra became aware of a soft surface beneath her—her bunk, she realized—and the familiar presence of the sword, muted but vigilant in her mind.

«You drained yourself,» it observed when it sensed her awakening. «Protective magic demands more energy than destruction. Curious.»

Lyra opened her eyes, finding herself in her small cabin. The quality of light suggested several hours had passed; evening shadows stretched across the floor.

*You helped save him*, she thought, the realization striking her as significant. *You didn't have to, but you did.*

The sword's response came reluctantly. «The demonstration served strategic purposes. Blackwood now sees additional value in our capabilities.»

*That's not why you did it*, Lyra pressed, sensing something deeper beneath the pragmatic explanation. *You could have refused.*

A long silence followed, the sword seemingly at war with itself. Finally, it admitted, «The boy's survival... was not entirely without value to me. His potential remains intact. That is... preferable to wasteful death.»

Lyra smiled faintly, recognizing this halting acknowledgment as meaningful coming from an entity that had previously valued human life only as a means to an end. *That sounds almost like compassion.*

«Do not mistake tactical reassessment for sentiment,» the sword retorted, though with less conviction than usual. «The protection we offered required less energy than fighting would have afterward. It was efficient.»

*If you say so*, Lyra conceded, too tired to argue further. She sat up slowly, noting that someone had removed her boots but left her fully clothed. The sword rested on the narrow table beside her bunk, close enough that their connection remained strong.

A soft knock at the door preceded Nari's entrance. The younger girl carried a tray with bread, dried fruit, and a steaming mug of something that smelled strongly of herbs.

"You're awake," she said, setting the tray on the edge of the bunk. "Good. We're approaching the outer islands. Blackwood wants you on deck within the hour."

Lyra accepted the mug gratefully, finding the contents bitter but invigorating. "How's the boy?"

"Terrified of both punishment and you." Nari's mouth quirked into a half-smile. "Being saved by magic isn't a common experience for fisher children."

"He shouldn't have been punished for climbing the rigging," Lyra said, frowning. "He's just a child."

"He wasn't." Nari settled on the edge of the bunk. "Blackwood intervened personally. Said his 'adventurous spirit' could be useful with proper training."

«She courts your favor,» the sword observed. «Using mercy as currency.»

Nari seemed to reach a similar conclusion. "Don't be fooled by occasional kindness. It's calculated, like everything she does."

Lyra nodded, eating quickly to restore her energy. Outside the small porthole, she could see the archipelago properly now—a scatter of islands rising from the sea like the spines of some enormous submerged creature. The largest loomed directly ahead, its slopes covered in dense vegetation unlike any she'd seen near Stillwater.

"What do you know about these stone-shapers?" she asked between bites.

"Not much," Nari admitted. "They keep to themselves, trading occasionally but permitting few outsiders to their inner islands. They're said to have their own magic, tied to the earth rather than artifacts." She lowered her voice further. "They've repelled Blackwood twice before. Something's changed if she's approaching directly now."

«The artifacts,» the sword supplied. «Three of the Four gathered provide leverage that even the most reclusive cultures would recognize. The stone-shapers particularly would understand the significance.»

Before Lyra could relay this information, the door opened without warning. Koric stood in the threshold, his expression more serious than usual.

"Time to go," he said simply. "Captain's waiting."

Nari stood, her face immediately resuming its careful neutrality. "I'll leave you to prepare."

As she passed Koric in the doorway, she brushed against him in what appeared to be an accidental collision. But Lyra noticed the barely perceptible nod they exchanged—a communication so subtle it might have been imagined.

«Interesting,» the sword commented. «Your friend has connections you're not aware of.»

Once Nari had gone, Koric remained in the doorway, watching as Lyra gathered herself and reached for the sword.

"What you did today," he said abruptly. "Saving the boy. I've served with Captain Blackwood for seven years and seen many strange things, but never an artifact of destruction used to preserve life."

Lyra buckled the sword's sheath onto her back, feeling the immediate strengthening of their connection as the blade settled into its customary position.

"Perhaps it was never meant to be only destruction," she suggested quietly.

Koric's expression revealed nothing, but his next words surprised her. "Be careful on the islands. The stone-shapers have their own understanding of the artifacts—older, perhaps, than Blackwood's. They may see you differently than she does."

With that cryptic warning, he stepped back, gesturing for her to precede him through the door.

The deck was a flurry of activity when they emerged. The *Tempest* had entered a natural harbor, its crew expertly maneuvering between rocky outcroppings that could tear the hull open if struck. On the shore ahead, torches flickered against the gathering dusk, illuminating stone structures integrated so naturally with the landscape that they seemed to grow from the island itself.

Captain Blackwood waited at the bow, resplendent in what must have been her ceremonial attire—a deep crimson coat with black embroidery, tall boots polished to a mirror shine, her braided hair adorned with small golden charms that caught the torchlight. Beside her stood Vex and Ilaria, similarly attired in formal clothing that emphasized their roles as artifact wielders.

"Ah, our final representative," Blackwood greeted Lyra, her assessing gaze noting every detail of her appearance. "You've recovered your strength. Good. Tonight's negotiations require a show of unity and power."

«Unity is a convenient fiction,» the sword commented privately. «She would dispose of us all if it served her purpose.»

"What exactly are we negotiating for?" Lyra asked, aware that this was the first time Blackwood had framed their interaction with the islanders as a negotiation rather than a conquest.

"The stone-shapers possess not only the Root of Stone, but knowledge of how to properly bond it to a worthy vessel." Blackwood's tone became almost reverent—unusual for a woman who typically spoke of power in coldly practical terms. "Their current guardian is dying. Tradition dictates that a successor must be chosen before death, or the artifact's power may be lost for generations."

"And you've arranged to provide this successor," Lyra guessed.

"Not I." Blackwood's smile was triumphant. "You did."

Before Lyra could question this cryptic statement, movement on the shore caught her attention. A procession had formed, figures carrying elaborate stone lanterns that cast eerie green-white light across the beach. At their center walked a tall figure leaning on a staff that appeared to be made of the same living stone as the buildings.

"Elder Kosai," Blackwood identified the central figure. "Guardian of the Root and leader of the eastern archipelago tribes." She straightened, assuming the posture of command that had made her reputation as the Pirate Queen. "Remember your role, Lyra. Observe, demonstrate when asked, but speak only when directly addressed."

The ship eased toward the stone dock extending from the beach. As they drew closer, Lyra could make out details of the waiting delegation—men and women with dark hair and broad features, their clothing woven in patterns that seemed to mimic the natural rock formations of their island. Many wore stone ornaments that gleamed with the same faint luminescence as their lanterns.

«They infuse ordinary stone with traces of earth magic,» the sword explained, its tone unusually respectful. «An ancient practice predating even the artifacts' creation.»

*You know them?* Lyra questioned, surprised.

«I know of them. The stone-shapers provided materials for the Archmage's forge when the artifacts were created. Their connection to earth magic made them natural allies in the balancing of elemental forces.»

This glimpse into the sword's true history intrigued Lyra, but there was no time to explore it further. The *Tempest* had reached the dock, and crew members were securing lines with practiced efficiency.

"With me," Blackwood commanded, leading her small delegation toward the gangplank.

As they descended to the dock, Lyra felt a subtle change in the sword's presence—a heightened alertness tinged with what might have been anticipation or anxiety. Its usual confidence seemed tempered by the proximity to this place where, perhaps, it had historical connections deeper than she understood.

«The Root of Stone lies ahead,» it confirmed. «I can sense its presence—dormant but aware.»

Elder Kosai stepped forward as they approached, his weathered face impassive beneath a crown of intricate stone beads. He carried himself with dignity despite the obvious pain each movement caused him.

"Captain Blackwood," he greeted in accented but clear common tongue. "You return to our shores with more artifacts than before." His gaze swept over the three wielders, lingering longest on Lyra. "And more interesting companions."

Blackwood bowed with surprising deference. "Elder Kosai. Thank you for granting us audience. As promised, I bring potential vessels for your consideration."

For the first time, Lyra noticed a figure standing slightly behind their group—a young boy with bright red hair. With a shock, she recognized him as the child who had delivered clothes to her cabin days ago, and whom she had saved from falling just hours earlier.

«Ah,» the sword's understanding dawned simultaneously with hers. «The boy's "adventurous spirit" indeed. Blackwood plays a longer game than we realized.»

Elder Kosai beckoned them forward. "Come. The Root grows restless as my time shortens. We will test your candidates within the Stone Circle."

As they followed the procession away from the docks, the sword's voice came to Lyra with unusual solemnity.

«Prepare yourself, young vessel. What happens next will change many fates—perhaps even our own.»

For once, Lyra found herself in complete agreement with the sword. Whatever game Blackwood was playing, they had just advanced to a more dangerous level, with consequences that would extend far beyond this remote island shore.
